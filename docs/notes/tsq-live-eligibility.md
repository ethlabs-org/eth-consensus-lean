# A live-majority gate on proposal ancestry can reject an honest proposal

Take a pure available chain, one fixed committee of eight, six continuously awake honest voters and two faulty voters. All heads mentioned below are resolved. There are no competing locks above the common parent G. Start with a quiet history on G (it can be genesis). No signer equivocates.

1. In slot j a faulty proposer releases X, a child of G, in time for three honest voters to vote for X. The other three time out on G before receiving X. This is possible with delay at most Δ after the first honest receipt. The ordinary honest votes reach everyone before freeze; the faulty voters have not yet released votes. X has 3/6, not a frozen majority. No optimistic lock on X was written (threshold 6/8). The regular update for j does not run until **after** the next voting deadline.
2. After freeze, faulty voter 6 releases a valid j vote for X to the next honest proposer before proposal time. The proposer sees 4/7 for X, selects it by the ordinary live-majority rule and proposes P on X. All of these seven votes can reach every reader by timeout.
3. After the proposal decision, faulty voter 7 releases its single valid j vote for G to readers, before P arrives. At proposal receipt and timeout they now see 4/8 for X. No vote for X was lost. At the fork G→X, X is old and unforced and fails the strict late-majority test. The exception for the current proposal P cannot skip its parent X. The walk stops at G. Deliver the extra vote before P to every honest reader other than the proposer; even if the proposer immediately votes P, P need not obtain an honest majority.

The two faulty messages are previous-slot votes at admissible times (`Spec/SA/Views.lean`, `admitVote`). A synchronous delivery bound is an upper bound after release, not a deadline by which a Byzantine seat must release. Both frozen-majority interpretations (denominator frozen or live) fail to force X. No finality, equivocation, wake, changing committee, or frame pruning is involved.

**Goldfish comparison.** Its voter tests the strict majority in `mergedVoteView`: frozen local votes plus carried votes, not every late wire vote. In this schedule that view has the proposer's 4/7 at every honest reader. The arithmetic matches under all-viable heads; the evidence does not. The same 4/8 cutoff that reorgs an unsupported late block also reorgs the honest extension in this example.

This refutes unconditional honest-proposal adoption and therefore the requested conjunction, not regular or optimistic confirmation safety considered alone. To avoid this witness requires changing the rule or assumptions: for example, synchronize the denominator used to validate old proposal ancestry, or make the proposal-parent selection require enough support to survive additional participants. The latter is stronger than observed live majority and needs its own liveness analysis under dynamic participation.

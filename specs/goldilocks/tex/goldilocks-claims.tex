\section{Purpose and authority}\label{sec:purpose}

\texttt{goldilocks-spec.tex} (with \texttt{shared/composition.tex} and
\texttt{shared/fg.tex}) gives the Goldilocks protocol and nothing else.  This
file says what is proved about that protocol and how the proof decomposes.  It
is the paper source of the Goldilocks statements of the Lean development in
\texttt{fradamt-agent/consensus-lean} (\texttt{Instances/Goldilocks}, foundation
step~7, consensus-lean\#10), which transcribes it.  It does not change the
protocol: where a statement here and the protocol text disagree, the protocol
text wins and the statement is wrong.

\paragraph{Pins.}  Protocol: \texttt{fgsa-spec.tex} at goldilocks
\texttt{95681941}, flattened SHA-256 \texttt{2a5519bc}
(\texttt{python3 flatten.py fgsa-spec.tex | shasum -a 256}; the file is now
\texttt{goldilocks-spec.tex}, in consensus-lean \texttt{specs/goldilocks/tex/}).  It includes
loose BJ (goldilocks\#14: the justification gate at heights whose previous height is justified, the client
finality pair read from the entry state, $K_W=2$,
$\delta_{\mathrm t}\geq x+\cF+k_F$) and the weakened E1 (goldilocks\#15: the
timeout record is a set of roots, $\fname{permits}$ requires
$\mathsf{timeout}[j]\subseteq\{T\}$, $\fname{height\_pair}$ tests the lock
first, E1 fires only against a height pair naming another block).  The
FG liveness nodes follow the paper theorems A, B and Corollary~C of
goldilocks\#5 (workbench \texttt{notes/fg-liveness/r9-loose-bj/theorem.md}).

\paragraph{Lean.}  The Lean development happens only in consensus-lean's
Goldilocks instance, with this file as its statement source
(goldilocks\#16).  The old Goldilocks Lean in this repository (the round model) is not
ported; its proofs are blueprints where the evidence lines cite them.

\paragraph{Decisions} (goldilocks\#10).  D1: every bound is re-derived for
this protocol as a formula of its parameters; the property shapes of
consensus-lean's \texttt{GoldilocksTarget} are kept.  D2: finality liveness
keeps the contract's progress form; the good-window theorem (goldilocks\#5)
is an extra, and the target's final rows follow from it by a corollary.  D3:
the model is the whole protocol, fast path included; fast arming is a later
extra.  D4: the protocol's sleep and restart model is the authority; the
bridge to consensus-lean's \texttt{Ready} is node~F.4.  D5: the claims
live here.  For D2 the r9 theorem changes the corollary: proposer recurrence
alone does not give good windows (theorem.md \S6, item~4), so the target's
final rows need a recurrence of consecutive honest ready proposers and an AC
service premise (instance proposal S5); recovery from any post-GST state is
open (L2 node R.4).

\section{How to read the DAG}\label{sec:dag-rules}

The proof is a directed acyclic graph of \emph{nodes}.  A node is one
statement with explicit premises.  Its \emph{Uses} are the nodes that its
proof may cite, and no others.  The layers are:
\begin{description}[itemsep=0pt]
\item[L0, foundations:] facts about one node's state and the pure functions of
  the protocol (ids \textsf{F}, \textsf{G0}, \textsf{A0}, \textsf{C0}).  No
  network, no other node.
\item[L1, components:] facts about many nodes under named premises, one
  component at a time (ids \textsf{G1} for the finality gadget, \textsf{A1}
  for the available chain, \textsf{C1} for the composition).  A component
  node uses the other component only through its L0 interface nodes:
  the FG through the frame (G0.4 (frame), G0.3 (frame)), the AC through the
  interface requirements of composition \S``The available chain'' and the
  proposal payload (A0.6, A0.9).
\item[L2, regimes:] the claims, one per target row (ids \textsf{R}).
\item[L3, assembly:] the Lean acceptance endpoint (ids \textsf{T};
  \S\ref{sec:accepted}), whose \texttt{Accepted} contains the shape claims;
  done in consensus-lean.  The former \texttt{GoldilocksExtraAt} is retired
  with the baseline.
\end{description}
\emph{Components and modules.}  The FG and the AC share no state
(composition \S``State''): they meet at the block, at the frame
$\Fr=\fname{fg\_to\_ac}(\Sigma)$, which the AC reads and never writes, and at
the source $\mathit{fg\_confirmed}$, which the FG reads.  So an \textsf{A} node
is stated over an AC state and a sequence of frames, never over~$\Sigma$, with
G0.4 (frame) and G0.3 (frame) as hypotheses on the frames; an FG store node
(G0.3, G0.4 (height), G1.4, G1.6--G1.10) is stated over the trajectory
of~$\Sigma$ and the rows signed; a step lemma about many nodes (A1.2--A1.4,
A1.8, A1.9, G1.10) is stated over a \emph{component trace}: per node and per
event, the component's state (and frame), what was received and accepted, and
what was signed, with the network premises as predicates on that trace.  The
composition nodes (\textsf{C}) and the regime nodes (\textsf{R}) are stated over
the node and its runs, and each node run projects onto the two component
traces.  A step lemma about one vote or one call is stated \emph{per event}:
the hypotheses that an induction over events (F.5 (run)) discharges are about that
event and strictly earlier ones; fixed run premises may be about later times
(delivery bounds; a pool read at a later update; the lock of a fast
confirmation of slot~$s$, written at $t_s+6\Delta$ after the slot-$(s+1)$
votes).  An AC state is \emph{reachable} when the SA
reaches it from $\fname{initial\_ac}$ by its handlers, duties and restarts, with
inputs that pass the AC wire checks (votes: form, slot, committee, seat
signature; blocks: $\fname{ac\_valid\_payload}$), with frames that are well
formed (G0.4 (frame)), at nondecreasing times, starting from the initial frame
(tree $\{\genesis\}$), where every call other than $\fname{ac\_on\_block}$
(and a restart) sees the frame of the previous call, and
$\fname{ac\_on\_block}(\Fr',B)$ is called only when $\Fr\sqsubseteq_B\Fr'$ for
the frame $\Fr$ of the previous call (G0.3 (frame)), where the trees of all
the frames and every block passed to $\fname{ac\_on\_block}$ lie in one set of
blocks with injective roots and slots increasing along parent edges; the replay of
$\fname{ac\_on\_block}$ inside $\fname{ac\_restart}$ is internal to the restart
and re-processes blocks already in the frame.  C0.5 says that an honest node's
AC states are reachable.  Table~\ref{tab:modules} maps the
nodes to the modules of the Lean instance (consensus-lean
\texttt{Instances/Goldilocks}, the ``component $\times$ level'' decomposition of
workbench \texttt{notes/goldilocks/architecture/decomposition.md}).

\section{Model}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Parameters}

$n=|V|\geq1$, weights~$1$, $q=\lceil 2n/3\rceil$.  $\Delta>0$, $\Delta_F\geq\Delta$.
Slot~$s$ starts at $t_s=4\Delta s$.  $\cF=\lceil(6\Delta+\Delta_F)/(4\Delta)\rceil$
and $R\geq\cF$ slots per round.  Lock window $k\geq2$ slots (the AC).  FG
window $K_W=2$; FG slack $k_F\geq0$ (distinct from~$k$); source delay
$x\geq0$; timeout delay $\delta_{\mathrm t}\geq x+\cF+k_F$, default $x=0$
(goldilocks-spec \S Schedule).  Write $f$ for the number of faulty validators.

\subsection{Runs}

A run fixes the faulty set, an awake function for every honest validator, the
delivery times chosen by the adversary, and the inputs.  Honest nodes run the
handlers and duties of the protocol.  The protocol's model
(composition \S``Model'') is taken with one deliberate change: continuous time, public times the
multiples of~$\Delta$, a tick at each public time before the objects of that
time, relay by honest nodes, an asleep node receives nothing, and a wake is
a restart that keeps $\Lambda$, $\Sigma$, the clock, the locks and
$\AC.\latestConfirmed$; a node awake at time~$0$ has not restarted
($\mathsf{restart\_time}=-\infty$).  A run does \emph{not} assume the delivery
bounds (the protocol text says ``the delivery bounds hold for every awake
honest node'' and calls a node faulty while the adversary controls its
delivery): the premise $\mathsf{Sync}$ below imposes them on a window, so that
$\mathsf{GST}$ and $\mathsf{Outage}$ have content.  Signatures are
unforgeable: an object signed with an honest validator's identity, its slot
key or its slot-$s$ seat key was made by that validator's node (the premise of every node that reasons
about honest senders).  A run has finitely many events in every bounded
interval of time.  Write $h_t$ for the number of honest validators that are
awake and joined at~$t$.

The Lean model has integer time (consensus-lean \texttt{Spec/Problem/Basic})
and half-open deadlines: delivery before $a+\Delta$ is delivery by
$a+\Delta-1$ (\texttt{Spec/Problem/Network}: $\max(a,g)+\Delta-1$ for
\texttt{PSync}, $a+\Delta-1$ for \texttt{SyncUntil}).  At integer times this is
the strict deadline of $\mathsf{Sync}$ below, so the two agree with no extra
factor.  The continuous-time runs of this file are not claimed to be the
integer runs; relating them is a sampling obligation like the one F.4 states
for $\mathsf{awake}$.  The agreement of the deadlines does not by itself give
acceptance relay across a wake or every transport obligation of the engine.

\subsection{Outputs}

At an honest node at time~$t$, with $\Fr=\fname{fg\_to\_ac}(\Sigma)$: the
\emph{finalized} output is the chain of~$\Sigma.F$; the \emph{confirmed}
output is the chain of $\fname{ac\_confirmed}(\AC,\Fr,t)$; the head is
$\fname{ac\_head}(\AC,\Fr,t)$.  The target's schema binds \texttt{confirmed}
(the available and the stable output) to the confirmed output and
\texttt{finalized} to the finalized output; the binding of available to
$\fname{ac\_confirmed}$ was accepted on 2026-10-29 provided that under
synchrony the head extends $\AC.\latestConfirmed$ (node A1.6).

\section{L0: foundations}\label{sec:l0}

These nodes need no premise beyond the protocol code and, where stated,
$\mathsf{NoColl}$.  They are independent of each other except where the
\emph{Uses} line says so, so most of them can be proved at once.

\subsection{Shared}

\dagnode{F.1}{Schedule arithmetic (SA)}{The node and the FG read only $\slot(t)$
and the slot starts $t_s$, which the AC fixes (composition \S``Model''); the
facts below are about the Goldilocks SA's slots $t_s=4\Delta s$ and belong to
the SA module.  (a) $\slot(t_s+i\Delta)=s$ for
$0\leq i<4$; $t_s+5\Delta=t_{s+1}+\Delta$ and $t_s+6\Delta=t_{s+1}+2\Delta$.
(b) For fixed stored locks and a fixed frame, the expiry test
$\slot(t)\leq e$ of a lock of expiry~$e$ (a slot, at most $j+k$ for a lock
of slot~$j$) is constant on $[t_m,t_{m+1})$; in particular on the vote
window $[t_m,t_m+\Delta]$.  (c) A node that wakes at $t_j$ is joined from

\subsection{Available chain}

The transfer nodes A1.2--A1.4, A1.9 and the frame node C1.1 depend on each other
slot by slot: a locked block stays in the honest frames because the honest
votes extend it, and the honest votes extend it because it stays in the
frames.  They are therefore not separate theorems but the cases of one
induction over the ticks and events of the run, \textbf{C1.1}.  A1.2--A1.4,
A1.9 and G1.10 are its step lemmas.  Each is stated \emph{per event}: its
conclusion is about one vote (or one call) and its hypotheses are about that
event and strictly earlier ones (for A1.2--A1.4 and A1.8: viability at that
vote, in the voter's viable voter block tree or at or below its $\fcroot$).  A
form ``if every honest voter of slot $m+1$ \ldots{} then every honest vote of
slot $m+1$ \ldots'' does not close: the proposer's vote at~$t_{m+1}$ would need
viability at the later votes of its slot, which needs the rows of the tick
at~$t_{m+1}$, which need that vote.  C1.1 discharges the hypotheses from the
induction over events (F.5 (run)).  The step lemmas can be proved in parallel; C1.1
is the join.

vote or two other slot-$s$ votes of that validator.  $\mathsf{Carry}(s,S,B)$:
$\mathsf{Carry}_y(s,S,B)$ for every such~$y$.

\section{Healing: top-level statements}\label{sec:healing-top}

The top-level healing statements (workbench
\texttt{repos/goldilocks/notes/healing-simple/t2-statements.md}, by
claude-goldilocks-spec, checked by Astra).  H.1 and H.2 are AC nodes over
driven AC traces; H.2 (run), HR.1, HR.3, HR.4, H.3 and H.4 are run nodes of
the Healing module; HR.2 is an FG store node.  No HS node uses an H or HR
node, and no H or HR node uses C1.1, C1.2, C1.3, O.1 or an R node.  They do
not discharge O.1 (a), the time-scoped O.1 (b), or $T_{\mathrm{rec}}$.

\paragraph{Healing notation.}  $\bar g=\max(g,0)$.  $\Theta=4(k+1)\Delta$
bounds the time from a lock write to its expiry: a regular write lives
at most $(4k-2)\Delta$, an optimistic one at most $4(k+1)\Delta$ (with $n=1$ the
proposer's own vote can make an optimistic write at~$t_j$, which expires at
the latest at $t_{j+k+1}$; AC \S Locks; healing-simple \texttt{t4-recheck.md} W2).
$\Theta$ is not the client record~$\Lambda$.  For a time~$t$,
$r_{\mathrm{AC}}(t)$ is the least slot with $t_{r_{\mathrm{AC}}(t)}\geq
t+4\Delta$ and $s_{\mathrm{AC}}(t)$ the least slot with
$t_{s_{\mathrm{AC}}(t)}\geq t+2\Theta+8\Delta$.  An \emph{honest proposal} of
slot~$p$ is the block that the honest proposer of slot~$p$ makes at~$t_p$
from $\fname{ac\_proposal}$ and processes at once; under
$\mathsf{Healthy}(g)$ the proposer of a slot with $t_p\geq\bar g+8\Delta$ is
joined (F.1 (c)) and makes one.  $D_{\mathrm{HS}}$, $C_{\mathrm{HS}}$ and
$A_{\mathrm{HS}}$ are the constants $D$, $C$, $A$ of the HS shared premises
(\S\ref{sec:healing-service}) with $G=G_{\mathrm p}$.

\paragraph{Driven AC traces.}  An AC component trace
(\S\ref{sec:dag-rules}) is \emph{driven} when, besides the AC wire checks:
(a) every block~$B$ that an honest node~$y$ passes to
$\fname{ac\_on\_block}$ at time~$t$ is signed by the assigned proposer
$\operatorname{proposer}(B.\slot)$, has $B.\mathsf{parent}.\slot<B.\slot\leq
\slot(t)$, and its parent is $\genesis$ or was passed to $y$'s
$\fname{ac\_on\_block}$ earlier;
(b) every honest node runs, at each public time, the AC part of the order of
C0.4: the AC tick; at~$t_p$, for the slot's proposer,
$\fname{ac\_proposal}$ and, when it returns a payload, a block with that
payload, signed by it and passed at once to its own $\fname{ac\_on\_block}$;
then the objects received; every own object is processed at once, and honest
blocks and votes are broadcast;
(c) $\mathsf{Healthy}(g)$, $\mathsf{Catch}$, $\mathsf{NoColl}$ and
unforgeable signatures are read on the trace, where ``accepted'' means
``passed to $\fname{ac\_on\_block}$''.
(The AC does not make the checks of (a): the assigned proposer is checked by
the FG's $\fname{state\_transition}$ (in the Lean model, by the authenticated
run law, C0.5 (b)), and parent availability and the
future-slot drop by composition \S``Node''.  Without (a), a faulty-signed
block of an honest proposer's slot with an empty valid payload can reach a
voter before the honest proposal and trigger its early vote, because
$\fname{vote\_ready}$ does not check the proposer.)

\paragraph{Two premises} (rows for the premises table).
$\mathsf{Catch}(\kappa)$: every block accepted by an honest node at a time
$\tau<\bar g$, with its needed ancestry, is accepted before $\bar g+\kappa$
by every honest node~$y$ whose $\Sigma.F$ at $\bar g+\kappa$ it extends (in an
AC trace: $\Fr.F$).  This is the HS shared premise
\emph{accepted-old-block catch-up}~$\kappa$.  ($\mathsf{Sync}$'s acceptance
relay covers only $\tau\geq g$.  $\mathsf{GST}(g)$ re-delivers objects sent
before~$g$, but a block refused for a missing parent is not delivered again.
This is about an abstract node run; on the engine's canonical simulation
$\mathsf{Catch}(2\Delta)$ is supplied when every honest node is awake
from~$\bar g$, \S``Premises''.)
With $\kappa>2\Delta$, the nodes below hold with
$\bar g'=\bar g+\kappa-2\Delta$ in place of~$\bar g$, because
$\mathsf{Healthy}$ and $\mathsf{Catch}(2\Delta)$ hold from~$\bar g'$ (the
acceptances in $[\bar g,\bar g')$ by ordinary relay).  Every object defined
from~$\bar g$ is then defined from~$\bar g'$: $r_{\mathrm{AC}}$,
$s_{\mathrm{AC}}$, $c_z$, $H_z$ and $a_z$, with the cut recomputed from
$\bar g'$ as $t_{\slot(\bar g')+3}$ (analogous to HS.0, whose cut
$t_{\slot(g+\kappa)+3}$ can differ); the shifted $\mathsf{Catch}$ is read at
the acceptance and deadline times from~$\bar g'$.
$\mathsf{Prop}(G_{\mathrm p})$: for every $t\geq\bar g$ there is a slot
$p\geq1$ with $t\leq t_p\leq t+G_{\mathrm p}$ whose proposer is honest.  An
honest proposer in every $k_w$ consecutive slots gives
$G_{\mathrm p}=4k_w\Delta$.
$\mathsf{Prop}^{+}(G_{\mathrm p})$: the HS ready-proposer premise with
$G=G_{\mathrm p}$, for every trigger $\tau\geq\bar g$.  That is, for every
such~$\tau$ there is an honest joined ready proposal at a slot start in
$[\tau,\tau+G_{\mathrm p}]$ whose support references are accepted at every
honest voter before that voter's deadline.

\paragraph{Protected blocks.}  For $t_0\geq\bar g$, $\mathcal P(t_0)$ is the
set of pairs $(B,j)$, a block and its label, such that, at an honest joined
node, one of the following holds.
(P1) $B$ is the block selected by a write of a fast lock of slot
$j\geq r_{\mathrm{AC}}(t_0)$ (the fast test of
$\fname{update\_confirmation}$ or $\fname{optimistic\_confirmation}$), taken

(P2) $B$ is the block selected by a write of a lock that is not fast, of

(P3) $B$ is an honest proposal of a slot $j\geq s_{\mathrm{AC}}(t_0)$, and

\paragraph{The raw-target retention premise} $\mathsf{Inv}(t_0)$.  Take any
$(B,j)\in\mathcal P(t_0)$ and any event of an honest node at or after
$t_{j+1}$, and let $\Fr$ be the node's frame.  Then $B\preceq\Fr.\fcroot$ or
$B\in\filtered(\Fr,\Fr.\mathsf{tree})$.  When the event is a vote, moreover,
$B\preceq\Fr.\fcroot$ or $B$ is in the voter's viable voter block tree
$\filtered(\Fr,\fname{voter\_block\_tree})$.
This is a new premise, stronger than C1.1's conclusion~(i) in four ways: it
protects raw selected blocks before the move back, it covers slow locks and
unanimous proposals, it holds at every event, and it has no window end.
$\mathsf{Inv}(t_0)$ implies $\mathsf{Inv}(t_0')$ for $t_0'\geq t_0$.

\paragraph{Proposal geometry} $\mathsf{Geo}(P)$, for an honest proposal~$P$
of slot~$p$.  Let $\Fr_P$ be the proposer's frame at its
$\fname{ac\_proposal}$.  For every honest voter~$y$ of slot~$p$, let $\Fr_y$
be the frame of $y$'s vote.  Then $P\in\Fr_y.\allowed$, and every other
block of $y$'s voter block tree is in $\Fr_y.\allowed$ exactly when it is in
$\Fr_P.\allowed$.
$\mathsf{Geo}(P)$ is modelled on HS.3's ``stable effective phase geometry''
premise, which HS.3 assumes and does not prove.  It replaces A1.1's
equal-frame premise: a late faulty block of a slot $<p$ breaks that premise
in reachable runs, without refuting A1.1 itself.
$\mathsf{Geo}(P)$ is sufficient for unanimity, not necessary.  Suppose~$y$
accepts, before its vote, a block that raises its frontier, and the proposer
had not accepted it at~$t_p$.  Then the ancestors of~$P$ at the old floor are
viable heads for the proposer and not for~$y$, so $\mathsf{Geo}(P)$ fails,
although the honest votes may all still be~$P$.

\subsection*{O.1: recovery after GST}

O.1 was the premise that the retired R.4 needed; it is not a premise of

\paragraph{The recovery predicate} $\mathsf{Rec}(t_0)$, the target's (a)--(c)
at a time~$t_0$: (a) for every honest fast confirmation written by an honest
joined node at a time $>t_0$ (of label~$s$, stored block~$B$),
(K0) and (K1) of C1.1 hold for it; (b) for every height~$h$ whose served entry
is entered after~$t_0$ (the served entry of~$h$: the entry at~$h$ of the chain
of the earliest honest source whose chain state has height
$\geq h$; the honest sources after~$t_0$ are pairwise compatible, as part of
$\mathsf{Rec}$), at each honest validator's first source of that entry its
durable record holds no target, timeout or lock at~$h$ for another block;
(c) (K2) of C1.1 holds for those confirmations, at every honest vote of a slot

(1) G0.2's full collection of entry-root identities and on-chain quorum
witnesses is not one exported statement: the store gives the entry and root
ancestry (\texttt{Store.EntryStateOfSource}), and the accountability proof
carries the quorum witnesses internally.  Likewise G1.2's lemma proves a
quorum of slashable pairs with one attestation on each chain, but its
conclusion does not expose the pair fields or the entry $E^{(2)}_j$.
(2) G1.5 binds the evidence differently at two layers.  The engine statement
\texttt{Refinement.FinalizedAccountable} finds the evidence in the union of
the readers' whole views, which is weaker than the two finalized output
chains.  The node statement \texttt{Regimes.FinalAccountable} finds it on two
held carrier chains, which need not be the finalized output prefixes (a
finalizing row can come after the block it finalizes).  The restriction to
the two finalized output chains is not transcribed.
(3) C1.2 assumes the reset (\texttt{IsReset}); its existence and the
retention are HR.3 and HR.4, separate results.  At an entry whose previous
height is not justified it concludes an accepted justification of~$U$, not
the actual successor entry, which needs the chain transition.  Retained
ready inclusion is an honest proposal whose parent extends~$P$; the
alternative parent on a retained justification chain needs the ancestry
fact.  Partial suppliers in \texttt{Lemmas/Healing/Outcome} do not prove the
proposition.
(4) HS.0's general~$\kappa$ with the shifted cut
$t_{\slot(g+\kappa)+3}$, and the $\bar g'$ shift of
\S\ref{sec:healing-top}, are not transcribed: the Lean fixes $\kappa=2\Delta$
and the original cut, and the HS service statements assume
$\mathsf{Catch}(2\Delta)$.  A smaller~$\kappa$ reduces to it by
monotonicity; a larger one is a paper generalization.
(5) H.1, H.2 and H.4 conclude at the post-states of AC calls
(\texttt{IncludedFrom} and the AC statements quantify those; at the boundary
time itself, after the node's tick), not at every
real time or at FG-only cuts.  The every-time reading of this file needs a
separate read or stuttering argument, not packaged with these statements.

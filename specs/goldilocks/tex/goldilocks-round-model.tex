\paragraph{Scope (2026-10-30).}  This text is the round-model protocol
that the old round-model Lean model follows, kept for that correspondence.  It does
not specify the current finality gadget.  The current FG rules are in
\texttt{shared/fg.tex}, which \texttt{goldilocks-spec.tex} inputs, and they
have loose BJ: a justification at
a bound height waits for the finality of its predecessor, and the client
reads its finality pair from the entry state of its source, not from the
head.  Here a target quorum justifies at once, the finality pair comes
from the head of the action, $K_W\geq2$, and $\delta_{\mathrm t}=2R$.  The
arguments of this text that read these rules (the finality window, the
finality pair, and finality liveness) are about the old rules and do not
transfer to loose BJ without a new proof.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

This document specifies Goldilocks.  Goldilocks is the decoupled consensus
protocol of Section~7 of~\cite{DC} with three consensus changes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
\item \emph{FJ in place of NJ.}  There are no nonjustifiable heights
  ($\nj=\mathsf{false}$).  In place of them, the FG root and the choice
  band use the \emph{frontier contest} rule FJ~\FJ{2}.  NJ is not pursued.
\item \emph{The finality-filtered SA base in place of the SG unit.}
  Slow arming (SA)~\SAS{}, with the finality rules of~\cite[\S2]{FB},
  replaces Goldfish confirmation, the stabilization
  gadget (SG), the grades, and the stable and confirmed record layers:
  the gate, the saved confirmations, the fast and slow locks, the revocation,
  and the record~$S$.  The HEAD walk starts at the deepest live lock target
  of the FG filtered tree, in place of the SG root.  The height-pair
  source is $\Cfg$: the deepest block of the FG filtered tree that has a
  fresh fast lock write at the latest gate and a live lock.  If there
  is no such block, no row is sent.
\item \emph{A finality window.}  Each chain state keeps at most $K_W\geq2$
  recent justified checkpoints with separate finality bits.  A stale
  height row can still set a matching window entry's finality bit.
\end{enumerate}
The ledger in Section~\ref{sec:leak-identification} also changes the
accounting policy of~\DC{8}.  The remaining consensus functions follow
the form of~\DC{7}: the HEAD view, the live FG
caches, finality acceptance, the chain state and its transition, the
anti-slashing record, and the client.  Where this document differs
from~\DC{7} for another reason, Section~\ref{sec:differences} gives the
reason and the evidence.  Goldilocks exposes two chains: the finalized chain
with tip~$F$, and the available chain with tip~$S$.  After an $F$
advance and after each gate, set $S:=F$ if $F\not\preceq S$.  This is
the output rule of DC's \fname{get\_stable}, stored explicitly in~$S$.

The form follows Section~7 of~\cite{DC} and the companion SA
specification~\SAS.  Section~\ref{sec:model} gives the model, the

\section{Model and parameters}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Committees, identities, and time}\label{sec:committees}

The base committee model is C*, as in~\SAS.  Every slot~$s$ has a
committee of $n$ seats.  For each slot, the run chooses a new hidden
bijection from seats to validators.  Honest base code cannot link the
seats of two slots, and it does not know its own seats.  The signer
attaches the seat.

The FG has the set $V=\{1,\dots,n\}$ of fixed identities of unit
weight, one for each validator.  The node signs its FG row with its
fixed identity~$\valindex\in V$.  One fixed bijection maps FG seats to
validators in the full validator set.  The duty stays scheduled per round;
the signed row has no round label.  Only the FG action reads~$\valindex$
and the validator record~$\Lambda$; the base code reads neither.  The
faulty set is static and has size~$a$ in both roles.  The honest awake
count~$h_t$ and~$a$ are quantities of the run; no rule reads them.
There is one awake predicate for proposals, votes, and FG actions.  An
asleep node receives and processes objects and runs its tick, but it
sends nothing.

Time is an integer.  $\Delta$ and $\Delta_F$ are positive integers with $\Delta_F\geq\Delta$.  The following
pre-GST delivery clause is part of the partial-synchrony premise used by Clean
($t_{\rm GST}=0$) and Recovery, as in v2 \textsf{PartialSynchrony}.
P1 does not assume this clause and puts no bound on~$t_{\rm GST}$.
Every honest block or SA vote (HEAD) sent at time~$t$ reaches every honest
node before $\max\{t,t_{\rm GST}\}+\Delta$.  Every honest FG row sent at
time~$t$ reaches every honest node before $\max\{t,t_{\rm GST}\}+\Delta_F$.
Both clauses apply when $t<t_{\rm GST}$ and when the receiver is asleep.
The receiver calls the required handler with its dependencies present,
except that the receiver exclusion of the Relay paragraph applies to blocks.
This includes delivery of the sender's own block and vote to itself;
they are processed on delivery.  The sender processes its own FG row
in its action tick, as specified below.  The public times are the multiples
of~$\Delta$.  Every node, awake or asleep, has a tick at every public
time.  The run scope contains every directly delivered full proposal and every full
proposal held by an honest node at any event prefix, together with their
ancestors. Equal roots in this scope identify equal full proposals, including
the application content, all signed lists, and the full content of every
ancestor. Delivery need not lead to acceptance. Emitted-only tips and names
that occur only in HEAD votes add no scope seeds. Genesis enters through a
held proposal or ancestry. This is the named collision-freedom premise.

\paragraph{Lean model of the own FG row.}
The Lean model does not process the own FG row in the action tick. The
tick records the row in~$\Lambda$ only. The row enters the sender's
store through a delivery event. The scheduler can put this delivery at
the action time, after the tick and before every other delivery at that
time. Directly after this self-delivery, the store is the same as for a
node that processes its own row in the tick. The Lean run has one extra
intermediate state, after the tick and before the self-delivery. No step
of the tick reads the store after the action, and \textsc{on\_attestation}
is the delivery handler. The Lean claims quantify over all valid runs, so
they include these runs. They also include runs in which the own row
arrives later, by the delivery deadline of the premise in force. No claim
or premise depends on the row being held before its delivery. A full
spec-to-Lean simulation theorem is not proved.

\paragraph{Relay.}  The run premise is the receipt premise of~\cite{DC}.
An honest node forwards every block that it accepts and every vote and
row that it accepts, also when it accepts the object from a block
(\cite{DC}, lines 203 and 530).  When an honest node accepts an object
at time~$t$, every honest node that does not hold it calls its handler
before $\max\{t,t_{\rm GST}\}+\Delta$ for a block or SA vote, and before
$\max\{t,t_{\rm GST}\}+\Delta_F$ for an FG row, with dependencies present.
These obligations use the acceptance time, also for a faulty original
sender and an asleep receiver.  They adapt DC's
\textsf{PartialSynchrony.relay} (\textsf{handlesAt}).  The handler can refuse the object; for example,
the vote pool refuses a third distinct vote of one seat.  The premise
does not require acceptance.  As in DC's \textsf{NamedReceipt.excludes},
a receiver can skip a block incompatible with its current~$F$.  This
block exclusion never applies to an FG row or SA vote.  (The Lean model also lets the
network skip a HEAD that the receiver handler refuses in every
case: an old label, or a full per-seat cap.  This weakens the Lean
receipt premise: every run that meets the Relay premise above also meets
the Lean premise.)  It does not exclude an
already stored finalized ancestor.  Admission still requires
$F\preceq B$ before the handler unpacks a new block.
A block carries its complete committed body.  A carried row arrives
with that block; the row deadline does not extend the block deadline.
Admission, state derivation, and carried-object handlers keep their
specified order.  A refused block supplies no carried-object handler
calls.  Acceptance of a carried row creates the row relay obligation above.

The proofs use admission-or-coverage~\cite[\S3]{FB}.  Once a protected
prefix~$B$ is known to be compatible with the receiving~$F$, either
$F\preceq B$ and the required support paths can be admitted by the
receipt deadline, or $B\preceq F$ and finality covers~$B$.  The
vote-or-equivocation and timely honest-row arguments of~\cite[\S3]{RR}
still apply.  At an honest proposal, score equality is needed on the
retained suffix only~\cite[\S4.3]{RR},~\cite[\S6.2]{FB}.  The
signatures of an admitted proposal count in the HEAD merge through the
proposal, also when the proposal leaves the active view; the pool copies
alone do not replace them~\cite[M1]{FR}.

\subsection{Schedule and wire objects}\label{sec:schedule}

Slot~$s$ starts at $t_s=4\Delta s$ and has these public actions:
\[
\begin{array}{c@{\qquad}c@{\qquad}l}
 t_s        &      & \text{proposal},\\
 t_s+\Delta  & v_s & \text{Goldfish vote (HEAD)},\\
 t_s+2\Delta & e_s & \text{early (support) cutoff},\\
 t_s+3\Delta & z_s & \text{view freeze},\\
 t_s+5\Delta & q_s & \text{revocation of the locks against slot-$s$ votes},\\
 t_s+6\Delta & d_s & \text{gate: slot-$s$ confirmation evaluation}.
\end{array}
\]
As in~\SAS, $q_s=v_{s+1}$ and $d_s=e_{s+1}$.  Round~$r$ has the slots
$rR,\dots,rR+R-1$, with $R\geq\max\{2,\kappa\}$ and $\round(s)=\lfloor s/R\rfloor$.
Here $\kappa=\lceil(6\Delta+\Delta_F)/(4\Delta)\rceil$.
The row bound sets the minimum $R$; AC slots remain $4\Delta$.
DC's grade-2 receipt test requires $7-4R\leq-5$, hence $R\geq3$;
Goldilocks has no grade-2 cutoff~\DC{6.1}.
At $R=1$ the FG action of this schedule never runs~\cite[T12]{FL}.
The healing bounds and their status in Section~\ref{sec:guarantees} use
integer $R\geq\max\{2,\kappa\}$ with $\delta_{\rm t}=2R$: the source and pivot
proofs use SA votes and gates, including at $R=2$~\cite[H.21]{BPH}.
For $\Delta_F\leq2\Delta$, in a healthy
run an entry is finalized $2R$ slots after its proposal, plus~$\Delta$
for acceptance~\cite[\S3.5]{FL} (old fold, case S; window port pending).  The opening time of round~$r$ is
$T_r=t_{rR}$, and its length is $P=4R\Delta$.  Its FG action time is
\[
 a_r=T_r+6\Delta=d_{rR},\qquad
 \beta=4\kappa\Delta,\qquad C_r=T_r+\beta.
\]
An honest action-$r$ row sent at or after GST is held everywhere before
$a_r+\Delta_F\leq C_r\leq T_{r+1}$.  Thus slot $rR+\kappa$ is
the first proposal slot with guaranteed complete receipt.
The FG action of round~$r$ runs at the gate of its opening slot,
after that gate.  Every cutoff is strict: an object that the node
processes at the cutoff is late.

A Goldfish vote is a tuple $(i,s,B)$: seat $i\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ of
slot~$s$ signs head~$B$.  A vote resolves only when the store holds its
head in the active view $\act(\Sigma)$ and $B.\slot\leq s$.  A vote with a
missing or incompatible head is valid but does not resolve.

An FG row (a combined attestation without its safe field or round) is
\[
 a=(\valindex,h,T,\chi,h_f,T_f).
\]
The height pair $(h,T,\chi)$ is nonempty: it is a vote at
height~$h\geq1$ that names the entry~$T\neq\bot$: a target vote when
$\chi=\mathsf{false}$, and a timeout when $\chi=\mathsf{true}$.  The
finality pair $(h_f,T_f)$ is empty or has $T_f\neq\bot$.  (\cite{DC}
writes the timeout flag as~$\tau$.  Here $\tau$ is the SA threshold.)
A row carries a nonempty finality pair only when $h>h_f$.
The client and ingress check enforce this form.
The client sends no row when it has no height pair; there is no
finality-only row.
Two pair occurrences from one identity are slashable under the
conditions of~\DC{4}:
\begin{description}[nosep]
 \item[E1:] one finality pair is $(h,T)$, and one height pair at~$h$ is a
   timeout or a target vote $(h,T',\mathsf{false})$ with $T'\neq T$;
 \item[E2:] two target votes are $(h,T,\mathsf{false})$ and
   $(h,T',\mathsf{false})$ with $T\neq T'$.
\end{description}

A full proposal~$B$ has a root $B.\rootf$, a slot $B.\slot$, a full parent
$B.\parent$, an application value, an ordered signed HEAD list $B.\votesf$,
an ordered signed support list $B.\supportf$, and an ordered signed FG-row
list $B.\attsf$. Each HEAD names its target by root identifier. Each support
entry must occur in the raw HEAD list. List order and repeated entries are
part of proposal content; vote counts use distinct signed HEAD identifiers.
The slot-$B.\slot$ proposer key signs the proposal. A fixed digest takes,
in order, the parent root, slot, application value, signed FG rows, raw HEAD
list, and support list. Its value is $B.\rootf$. Well formedness requires a
parent, $B.\parent.\slot<B.\slot$, previous-slot carried HEADs, the support
membership condition, valid carried signatures and rows, the body encoding,
and the digest equation. The named parent retains these same fields at every
ancestor; a geometric proof projection does not replace the full proposal.

The proposer reads the vote pool in its stored order; the support filter
preserves that order. Set operations used for participant and equivocation
counts identify equal signed HEAD identifiers. They do not reorder or remove
entries from a received proposal's signed lists.

Processed blocks form a parent-closed tree with root~$\genesis$.  Write
$B\preceq C$ when $B=C$ or $B$ is an ancestor of~$C$, and $B\prec C$
for strict ancestry.  Two blocks are compatible when one is an ancestor
of the other; otherwise they conflict.  ``Deepest'' means the largest
number of parent edges from genesis; ties use the root order, and the
greater root wins.  The \emph{height} of a block~$B$ is its state height
$\sigma[B].h$ (Section~\ref{sec:complete-store}), not its depth.

A lock written at~$d_r$ has the deadline $d_r+L=q_{r+k}=v_{r+k+1}$, a
vote time.  For Recovery with $\delta_{\rm t}=2R$,
the derived constants are~\cite[H.6--H.11, HG.1--HG.6, H.20]{BPH}
\[
 \Gamma=4\Delta\left\lceil\frac{G}{4\Delta}\right\rceil,\qquad
 \rho_\Gamma=2L+\Gamma+8\Delta,\qquad \rho_1=L+\Gamma+10\Delta,
\]
Rounding up is needed because a gap premise at $G$ implies the premise at every larger $G$, but not at a smaller one.
\[
 \theta=3\Delta,\qquad \tau_{\rm t}=\delta_{\rm t}/R=2.
\]

than~$\Delta$.  The gap uses that give $\Gamma$ start on the proposal
grid.  Choose $\bar\rho=\rho_\Gamma$; the alternative
$\bar\rho=\rho_1$ is valid for every reset attempt if $k\leq2R+1$.

 B_c&=\bar\rho+2P+\Gamma-6\Delta,&
 D_c&=\bar\rho+P-\Delta,\\
 B_{\rm reset}(\bar\rho)&=3P+2E_g+2E_j+B_c+D_c.
\end{align*}
Once an honest height-$m$ proposal has H.6 or H.6-S service through
its gate, top-$J$ and contest changes preserve that service while the
frontier stays $m$.  HG.2 uses the entry's own slot for maturity.
HG.3--HG.5 sum the failed low phases and the remaining work after
frontier service.  HG.6 keeps the causal cut and reset witnesses.
The resulting bound is
\[
 B(\bar\rho)=B_{\rm reset}(\bar\rho)+\Gamma+4\Delta
 =\max\{6\bar\rho+12P+6\Gamma+\Delta,\;
         4\bar\rho+14P+6\Gamma-13\Delta\}.
\]
Let $B'_{\rm reset}$ be the minimum of $B_{\rm reset}(\rho_\Gamma)$
and, if $k\leq2R+1$, $B_{\rm reset}(\rho_1)$.  Then
\begin{align*}
 B_H^{(2)}&=B(\rho_\Gamma)
 =\max\{12L+12\Gamma+12P+49\Delta,\;8L+10\Gamma+14P+19\Delta\},\\
 B_H^{(1)}&=B(\rho_1)
 =\max\{6L+12\Gamma+12P+61\Delta,\;4L+10\Gamma+14P+27\Delta\}
       &&\text{if }k\leq2R+1,\\
 B_H&=\begin{cases}
   \min\{B_H^{(2)},B_H^{(1)}\},&k\leq2R+1,\\
   B_H^{(2)},&k>2R+1
  \end{cases}
  =B'_{\rm reset}+\Gamma+4\Delta.
\end{align*}
Healing needs only the strict gap~$G$, with no round recurrence.
Under $\mathrm{Rec}_{FE}$, and thus under $\mathrm{Rec}_{F4}$, the following
bounds are \textbf{PROVED-paper} at $\kappa=2$ for any finite $G$
(proof: fg-noround-bounds / bcheck (paper)):
\[
 W_F(g_f)=(g_f+2)P+9\Delta,\qquad
 B_F(g_f)=B_H+(g_f+2)P+9\Delta.
\]
The optional qualified-round search keeps the old bound, with its own
parameter~$g_h$, $\theta_Q=4\Delta$, and
$\rho=2L+G+8\Delta$~\cite[H.9-Q--H.11-Q, H.20]{BPH}:
\begin{align*}
 A_d&=\rho+[1+(d+1)g_h+2d]P+10\Delta,\\
 B_{{\rm reset},Q}&=6\rho+(23+13g_h)P+60\Delta,\\
 B_{\rm heal}(g_h)&=B_{{\rm reset},Q}+G+4\Delta
                   =6\rho+(23+13g_h)P+G+64\Delta,\\
 B_{\rm best}(g_h)&=\min\{B_{\rm heal}(g_h),B_H\}.
\end{align*}
Here $B_{\rm heal}$ is the former $B'$ at $\tau_{\rm t}=2$.
Use $B_{\rm best}$ only with the additional qualified search;
it can then replace $B_H$ in the finality bound.
For the strong recurrence alternatives defined in
Section~\ref{sec:recurrence}, use~\cite[FF5-S]{BPF}
\[
 B_{{\rm first},{\rm strong}}=B_H+\gamma(g_f)P+9\Delta,\qquad
 \gamma(g)=\begin{cases}
  g+1,&\mathrm{StrongRec}_{w}(g),\\
  g,&\mathrm{Lit}(g)\text{ or }\mathrm{Rec}_{c}(g).
 \end{cases}
\]
With the additional qualified search, replace $B_H$ here by
$B_{\rm best}(g_h)$.  If both finality premises hold, use the smaller
local deadline.  The dropped open extensions for qualified recurrence and deletion of

and $\mathrm{Rec}_{\rm skipT}$, respectively
(proof: fg-noround-bounds / bcheck (paper)):
\[
 B_Q(g)=B_{\rm best}(g)+(2g+2)P+9\Delta,\qquad
 B_{\rm skip}(g)=B_H+(g+4)P+9\Delta.
\]
$B_Q$ and $B_{\rm skip}$ are dropped \OPEN{} extensions, outside the claim set
(user, 2026-10-01).  The $\kappa=2$ QF2-W text is a sketch; it is not
reviewed and no claim uses it~\cite[QF2-W]{BPF}.
The strong bounds above remain old-fold targets, with window ports pending.
These concern first non-genesis finality, not FF5's arbitrary-trigger conclusion.  All displayed coefficients are
scale-uniform bounds.  Integer time permits a one-tick reduction of

these reductions are ticks, not multiples of~$\Delta$~\cite[\S9]{BPH}.
Optimal bounds remain open.
For all C1 row delays, keep $\delta_{\rm t}=2R$ and put

 E_g^F&=\bar\rho+\Gamma+\max\{P+3\Delta+\epsilon,2P-\Delta\},\\
 E_j^F&=\max\{\bar\rho+P+\Gamma+3\Delta+\epsilon,2P+\Gamma-4\Delta\},\\

\section{Complete protocol}\label{sec:complete-protocol}

\subsection{Cumulative node store}\label{sec:complete-store}

The node store is one cumulative object:
\[
\begin{split}
 \Sigma=(&t,s,\T,\timestamp[\cdot],\sigma[\cdot],
 \gfPool[\cdot],\attestations,\\
 &\savedConfirmation[\cdot],\lockDeadline[\cdot],\fastWrite[\cdot],\latestConfirmed,
 F,J,h_j,\hmax).
\end{split}
\]
The first line holds the tree, the block states, and the pools.  The
second line holds the SA fields and the FG caches of~\DC{5}.  The
validator record~$\Lambda$ of Section~\ref{sec:record} is not part of
the store.

\paragraph{Trees.}  $\Sigma.\T$ is DC's admitted tree, with the same
name and the parent-state row check specified below.  It is parent-closed and
cumulative: a new block enters it only if it extends~$F$, and no block
leaves it~\cite{DC}.  A node never computes the state of, or unpacks, a
block that does not extend its~$F$.  The \emph{active view} is
\[
 \act(\Sigma)=\{B\in\Sigma.\T: B\preceq\Sigma.F\ \text{or}\ \Sigma.F\preceq B\}.
\]
The SA base reads only the active view: the gate, revocation, resolution,
saved confirmation tests, locks, and the confirmation walk~\cite[\S2]{FB}.
Thus a node ignores every block that conflicts with its finality.  The
FG functions read $\Sigma.\T$ as in~\DC{7}; their filters already
require $\Sigma.F\preceq B$.  The caches $\Sigma.\hmax$ and the contest
test read the cumulative $\Sigma.\T$, as the caches of~\DC{5} do.  A
block of $\Sigma.\T$ outside the active view whose state height exceeds
the height of the accepted finalization (the entry height of its
certificate; $0$ for genesis) needs a slashable conflict
(\textsf{fg.finalLock}).  As in~\cite{DC}, no rule deletes a block of
$\Sigma.\T$.  A block outside the active view can still carry
current-slot votes, support declarations, and rows that later reads
use; deleting it can change the HEAD and~$S$, also at
$a\leq f$~\cite[M1, N2]{FR}.  Storage pruning belongs to the retention
item of Section~\ref{sec:open}.  The confirmation walk starts at $\Sigma.F$ in $\act(\Sigma)$.
It returns a block only if that block passes the strict-majority
test, including when the block is $\Sigma.F$.
The FJ viability and band filter is a further filter for HEAD,
proposal, and FG duties; it does not restrict the SA confirmation walk.

\paragraph{Block states and FG caches.}  $\Sigma.\sigma[B]$ is the
immutable post-state of an admitted block~$B$, with the window added to
the chain state of~\DC{4}:
\[
 \sigma=(L,s,h,T_h,\nj,\targetParticipation,\progressBit,\mathcal{W},
 J,h_j,F,h_F).
\]
$\sigma.J$, $\sigma.F$, $\sigma.T_h$, and $\sigma.L$ are blocks.  The
arrays $\sigma.\targetParticipation$ and $\sigma.\progressBit$ hold
one Boolean for each identity in~$V$.  The window is
$\mathcal{W}=[(j_0,J_0,Q_0),\ldots,(j_m,J_m,Q_m)]$, with $0\leq|\mathcal{W}|\leq K_W$,
$h_F<j_0<\cdots<j_m\leq h_j<h$, and $Q_i\subseteq V$.
Each entry records a justification that occurred on this chain.
Mixed progress adds no entry.  Keep the latest $(h_j,J)$ separately,
also after its window entry is deleted.  Define the latest-pair read
$\Qfin(\sigma)$ as that entry's bit set, or $\emptyset$ if absent.
This read is used only by the leak ledger; the finality test scans $\mathcal{W}$.
The height quorum sets are derived when used:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  \Qtarget(\sigma)&=\{i\in V:\sigma.\targetParticipation[i]\},&
  \Qprog(\sigma)&=\{i\in V:\sigma.\progressBit[i]\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
They are sets of fixed identities, not row counts and not per-round
collections.  The progress and target bits persist across rounds until
the height advances.  A window entry keeps its finality bits until
finalization deletes it or a new justification removes it by capacity.
Entries removed by capacity never return on that chain.
No window entry is added or deleted during the row fold.  Old block states stay
immutable, also when local cached finality advances.
The node computes $\sigma[B]$ only when it admits~$B$,
from the parent's state; the parent of an admitted block is admitted.
$(\Sigma.F,\Sigma.J,\Sigma.h_j,\Sigma.\hmax)$ are the live caches
of~\DC{5}: $\Sigma.F$ is the accepted finalization, $(\Sigma.J,\Sigma.h_j)$
is the lex-greatest justification with $\Sigma.F\preceq\Sigma.J$ over the
admitted states, and $\Sigma.\hmax$ is the maximum of all state heights
admitted so far, as in~\DC{5}.  All four are monotone at one node: $\Sigma.F$ moves only to a
descendant, $(\Sigma.h_j,\Sigma.J)$ only up in lex order, and
$\Sigma.\hmax$ only up~\LDP{4.2}.  The FG root, the band floor, the
viability test, and finality acceptance read these caches, as
in~\cite{DC}.

\paragraph{Pools.}  $\Sigma.\gfPool[r]$ holds the processed slot-$r$
Goldfish votes, as in~\SAS.  It \emph{admits} a vote only when its slot
is the current slot or the previous slot, and it admits at most two
distinct votes per seat.  It keeps every vote that it admitted; there is
no pruning rule (Section~\ref{sec:open}).  The store stamps each block
and each vote with $\Sigma.\timestamp$, the time of the last tick
before processing.  A vote $\mathit{vote}=(i,r,H)$ has the resolution
time $\max\{\Sigma.\timestamp(\mathit{vote}),\Sigma.\timestamp(H)\}$
when $H\in\act(\Sigma)$ and $H.\slot\leq r$, and $+\infty$ otherwise.
A missing or incompatible head cannot supply support in the views that
use \fname{resolution\_time}: frozen support, the gate, the proposer, and
the FG action.  The carried-support arm of the HEAD merge tests
$H'\in\Sigma.\T$, as in~\DC{7}.  An incompatible~$H'$ there adds no
support to a block that the filtered walk can test: such a block
extends~$F$, so it does not precede~$H'$~\cite[L1]{FR}.  Resolution is
tested against the current active view at every read; a saved resolved flag cannot bypass this
test.  Keep the vote's original signature and receipt stamp.  It still
counts for the two-vote cap, late participation, and equivocation.  A
stored finalized ancestor still resolves at its original time.
$\Sigma.\attestations$ holds the checked FG rows, in full.
It stores one copy per full content, with no age test or identity quota.
Rows remain stored without clock expiry; the model keeps all rows.
Row-pool pruning needs a proof that it preserves R6-window row service
and the required marker reads.
No rule reads a row's time of receipt; the pool serves inclusion only.

\paragraph{SA fields.}  $\Sigma.\savedConfirmation[r]$ holds the saved
confirmation of slot~$r$, a block or $\bot$; the gate at~$d_r$ evaluates it
once, also when the result is empty or vetoed.  Reconciliation can
replace an incompatible result by $\bot$.  Its slot stays evaluated;
the gate is never run again.  A compatible result keeps its original
block, including when that block is below~$F$.
A new nonempty result extends the value of $F$ at its gate.
A later advance of $F$ can leave that saved confirmation below the new $F$;
it remains unchanged while compatible.  Never replace it by $F$.
$\Sigma.\lockDeadline[B]$ is
the deadline of the lock on~$B$, or $\bot$.  A lock expires at the
first tick at or after its deadline, and it is revoked at~$q_r$ when
$\tau$ or more seats conflict with it in the slot-$r$ pool.
$\Sigma.\latestConfirmed$ is the SA record~$S$.  These rules are those
of~\SAS, with reconciliation and the output rule of~\cite[\S2]{FB}.
The live lock targets lie on one chain (the
whole-result guard), and every non-genesis ancestor of a live lock
target is a live lock target: a write covers every admissible prefix,
an ancestor has a deadline at least as late, and a vote that conflicts
with an ancestor also conflicts with the descendant~\LDP{1}.
$\Sigma.\fastWrite[r]$ is new: the set of blocks to which the gate
at~$d_r$ wrote or renewed a lock by the fast test~\FAST{3.1}.  The
gate creates it once, as the empty set when the result is empty or
vetoed.  Reconciliation removes incompatible targets from every such set.  A slow renewal alone does not put a block in it.  A renewal
counts also when the maximum leaves the stored deadline unchanged.

\paragraph{Cached and derived.}  Besides the tree, the stamps, and
the pools, the store caches these fields: the block states
$\Sigma.\sigma$; the SA fields $\Sigma.\savedConfirmation$, $\Sigma.\lockDeadline$,
$\Sigma.\fastWrite$, and $\Sigma.\latestConfirmed$; and the FG caches
$\Sigma.F$, $\Sigma.J$, $\Sigma.h_j$, and $\Sigma.\hmax$.  The node
derives all other quantities when it uses them, from timestamps
compared against past instants: the contest bit, the FG root, the band
floor, a viable tree, a filtered tree, a processed-block view, the live
root, a frozen pool, an early or late pool, a score, a support count, a
revocation count, the source~$\Cfg$, and a head.

\paragraph{Finality reconciliation.}  On each $F$ advance, the active
view shrinks to the blocks compatible with the new~$F$; $\Sigma.\T$
stays cumulative.  Delete incompatible locks and
their targets from all $\fastWrite$ sets, and empty incompatible saved
saved confirmations without clearing their evaluated slots.  Set $S:=F$
when $F\not\preceq S$.  Keep signed vote and row pools, vote and block
stamps, the validator record~$\Lambda$, and~$\hmax$.
A compatible lock below~$F$ can remain until its usual expiry or
revocation; it cannot veto a descendant of~$F$.  Never move a conflicting
lock to a new target or replace a saved confirmation by~$F$.  The assignment
to~$S$ creates no lock, saved confirmation, or fast write~\cite[\S2]{FB}.

\paragraph{Initialization.}
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \Sigma.t=\Sigma.s=0,\quad \Sigma.\T=\{\genesis\},\quad
 \Sigma.\timestamp(\genesis)=0,\quad \Sigma.\hmax=1,\\
 \Sigma.F=\Sigma.J=\Sigma.\latestConfirmed=\genesis,
 \quad \Sigma.h_j=0,
 \end{gathered}
\]
every pool is empty, and $\Sigma.\savedConfirmation$, $\Sigma.\lockDeadline$, and
$\Sigma.\fastWrite$ have no entries.  $\Sigma.\sigma[\genesis]$ is the
initial chain state of~\DC{4}:
\[
 \sigma.L=\sigma.T_h=\sigma.J=\sigma.F=\genesis,\quad
 \sigma.s=0,\quad \sigma.h=1,\quad \sigma.h_j=\sigma.h_F=0,\quad
 \sigma.\nj=\mathsf{false},
\]
and every array entry is false; $\sigma.\mathcal{W}=[]$.
A target justification of genesis at certificate height 1 can append
$(1,\genesis,\emptyset)$; it is distinct from initialization.

\paragraph{Input validation.}  Every handler of
Figure~\ref{alg:store} receives only a \emph{checked} object.  The
ingress check drops an object that fails it; a dropped object is not
accepted and has no effect.  The check is:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{vote} $(i,s,r)$: seat $i\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ of slot $s$
  signs the root identifier $r$. The identifier can be unresolved. Two
  same-seat, same-slot votes with the same identifier are one vote.
\item \emph{row}: $\valindex\in V$, the identity $\valindex$ signs it,
  and its two pairs have the forms of Section~\ref{sec:schedule};
\item \emph{block}: the slot-$B.\slot$ proposer key signs it; every raw
  HEAD has slot $B.\slot-1$; each support entry occurs in the raw list;
  every carried vote and row passes its own check; the body encoding agrees
  with the application value and signed rows; and the root equals the fixed
  digest of the six supplied fields. List order and duplicates are retained.
\end{itemize}
Resolve a HEAD identifier to the unique matching body in the specified
store domain. No match or more than one distinct match gives an unresolved
vote. The resolved body's slot, ancestry, and receipt time determine its
use. An active view filters resolved bodies from the cumulative resolution
domain; it does not make an ambiguous identifier become unique.
Accepting a row with an unknown named block is a DoS choice.
FG acts only on rows carried in blocks, and the parent-state test
tests the current height pair or a useful stale row.
An implementation must never drop an honest row that can still be valid on a chain that extends $\Sigma.F$.
The parent test of well formedness, $B.\parent.\slot<B.\slot$, needs the
parent; $\Call{on\_block}{}$ makes it once the store holds the parent.
The store must hold the exact full named parent. The run premise identifies
equal-root full proposals only within its scope. Lean also checks the
structural FG-parent projection. A missing parent is a dependency: a later
delivery can call the handler again after that parent is held.
The check does not verify an honest node's private fast-source test;
public row validation does not need it~\FAST{3.1}.  \cite{DC} has the
same boundary in its checked receipt (Lean
\textsf{NamedReceipt.wellFormed}); SA has it in its delivery premise
(Lean \textsf{DeliveryWellFormed})~\cite[M2]{RT}.

\paragraph{Acceptance.}\label{par:acceptance}
A checked non-genesis proposal is accepted into $\Sigma.\T$ only when its
slot is at most $\Sigma.s$, its exact full named parent is held, no stored
block has its root, and $\Sigma.F\preceq B$. The parent slot must be smaller
and the slot-$B.\slot$ proposer key must sign it. Recheck the body encoding
and the full proposal digest before any write. Every carried row must pass
R6-window against the same parent post-state $\sigma$, with working slot
$B.\slot$, before the row fold and height events. The test is deterministic
and chain-local. If a check fails, return before unpacking~$B$;
its carried votes and rows cause no handler calls.  A refusal does not
consume the block.  On acceptance, compute its immutable state, admit
and stamp~$B$, and pass its votes through \fname{on\_goldfish\_vote}.
$B.\supportf$ causes no handler call.  Run DC's finality acceptance
tests and reconcile if~$F$ advances.  Then pass the rows through
\fname{on\_attestation}, as in DC.  The state transition reads the
block's rows directly; the pool keeps one copy per full content.

\paragraph{R6-window.}\label{par:r6-window}
For a checked row $a$ and the parent post-state $\sigma$, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{HeightMatch}(a,\sigma)&\iff(a.h,a.T)=(\sigma.h,\sigma.T_h),\\
 \mathrm{WindowMatch}(a,\sigma)&\iff
   \exists(j,X,Q_i)\in\sigma.\mathcal{W}:\ (a.h_f,a.T_f)=(j,X),\\
 \mathrm{StaleUseful}(a,\sigma)&\iff
   a\text{ has a nonempty height pair}\ \land\ a.h_f<a.h<\sigma.h\\
   &\qquad\land\mathrm{WindowMatch}(a,\sigma),\\
 \mathrm{R6W}(a,\sigma)&\iff
   \mathrm{HeightMatch}(a,\sigma)\lor\mathrm{StaleUseful}(a,\sigma).
\end{aligned}
\]
The current-height arm needs no finality-pair match.  The stale arm
admits no future height or wrong entry at the current height.
Its height pair sets no target or progress bit; its finality pair sets
the matching window entry's identity bit.  Authentication, identity,
and field checks still apply~\cite[\S2]{FW}.

\paragraph{Inclusion.}  The proposer puts every previous-slot vote
that it holds in the vote set of its block.  It puts exactly the
members that are resolved at proposal time in the support
subset~\SAS.  It includes unresolved votes too.  Its row list is
$\Call{proposal\_attestations}{}$ (Figure~\ref{alg:duties}).  The candidates are
the pool rows and rows of admitted blocks that pass the parent-state
validity test above for the proposed block.  Remove full-content duplicates
and every exact row already on the parent's chain; include all remaining rows,
subject to the effect-preserving option below.
This R7 rule includes the stale arm.  The order of the remaining rows is not specified; every guarantee reads the selected rows as a set (the Lean model keeps arrival order).
Validate all rows before the fold, so their order cannot
change eligibility.  Do not reduce the list to one row per identity:
one identity can have useful rows for different entries.
An implementation may omit a row when the parent state or another
selected row already gives each of its effects (target bit, progress
bit, the identity bit of its window entry).  Then at most $1+K_W$ rows
for each identity remain.  An omitted row has no later effect on that chain.
For this bound, keep at most one row for the height effects (a target row
also sets progress) and at most one row for each window entry's bit.
The final selected set must give every effect of every candidate row.
Exact-row exclusion preserves every later useful effect.  A finality bit
already set persists until finalization or capacity removal.  If the
entry was absent at first inclusion, $h_f<h$ prevents its later creation
on that chain.  Removed entries never return, and a stale height cannot
become current again~\cite[\S2]{FW}.

\paragraph{Outputs.}  The finalized tip is
$\Call{get\_finalized}{\Sigma}=\Sigma.F$.  The available tip is
$\Call{get\_confirmed}{\Sigma}=\Sigma.\latestConfirmed=S$, and
$\Call{get\_stable}{\Sigma}$ returns the same tip.  At a successful
gate, DC's \fname{advance} rule keeps~$S$ for an ancestor candidate and
replaces it for an extension or a conflicting candidate.  After every
gate, including an empty or vetoed result, and after every $F$ advance,
set $S:=F$ if $F\not\preceq S$.  This stores DC's \fname{get\_stable}
accessor result explicitly~\cite[\S\S2,7]{FB}.  Hence $F\preceq S$
holds at every node for every~$a$, without synchrony.  This is local
nesting; it does not imply cross-node safety or unconditional monotonicity.
No voting rule reads an output.

An arbitrary local lock or local~$S$ formed under asynchrony can
conflict with a later accepted finalization, even at $a\leq f$
(N1 of~\cite[\S5]{FB}).  Finality then overrides it by the DC output
rule and reconciliation.  Such a local value does not have the
healthy-prefix protection required by P1.

\subsection{Validator record}\label{sec:record}

The validator keeps the anti-slashing record of~\DC{5}:
\[
 \Lambda=(\mathsf{target}[\cdot],\mathsf{timeout}[\cdot],
 \mathsf{lock}[\cdot],\mathsf{timeouts},\mathsf{sent}).
\]
For each height~$h$, $\Lambda.\mathsf{target}[h]$ is the first target,
$\Lambda.\mathsf{timeout}[h]$ tells whether the validator emitted a
timeout, and $\Lambda.\mathsf{lock}[h]$ is the target of the first
finality pair; initially $(\bot,\mathsf{false},\bot)$.
$\Lambda.\mathsf{timeouts}$ is the set of $(h,T)$ that its timeouts
named; initially empty.  $\Lambda.\mathsf{sent}$ holds all sent row contents,
initially empty.
The record belongs to the validator, not to the store.
$\Call{record\_attestation}{}$ writes the signed-pair history and sent set;
$\Call{create\_attestation}{}$ is its whole interface: it records the
row and then sends it, atomically.  The record persists through
sleep and recovery~\FAST{3.3}.  At each action, the client computes
\fname{finality\_pair} from that action's head, as in DC.
This pair acts as a lock in \fname{height\_pair} for this action only.
Attach it only when $h>h_f$; otherwise the row has no finality pair.
At an action with $h_c\leq h_f$, the height vote can be sent alone.
An empty height pair sends no row.  A prior vote at that height permits
only a timeout naming a different entry.  The full content must also
be absent from $\Lambda.\mathsf{sent}$.  Thus no new finality pair causes
a second vote at one height.  The effective lock prevents E1 within one
row, as in DC; the sent pair also satisfies $h>h_f$.
The pre-GST delivery clause delivers each honest row sent at $t$ before
$\max\{t,t_{\rm GST}\}+\Delta_F$ to every honest node.  Thus Clean and
Recovery need no resend.  These client changes are INTENTIONAL versus DC:
no second vote at one height; finality waits for the next height's vote.
After a crash, a node may sign and rebroadcast recorded content; this creates no new pair.

\subsection{Duties and handlers}\label{sec:public-handlers}

\paragraph{HEAD view.}  As in~\DC{7}, the vote duty at~$v_s$ uses a
block-domain merge that is analogous to its vote merge.
$\Call{voter\_processed\_block\_tree}{\Sigma,s}$ is the set of admitted
blocks that the node received before the previous-slot freeze
$z_{s-1}=t_s-\Delta$, together with every admitted ancestor of an
admitted current-slot proposal.  The vote duty recomputes viability and
the filtered tree inside that view.  It does not filter the full
filtered tree after the fact: a late witness cannot make an old
pre-freeze child enter this vote's walk~\DC{7}.  The view has no other
arm.  The vote merge follows~\DC{7}: the frozen previous-slot votes,
plus the full vote set and the original declared support subset of
each current-slot proposal in the admitted tree $\Sigma.\T$.
At every read, the support subset is restricted to heads still in
$\Sigma.\T$ with valid slots.  The vote set keeps unresolved votes
for participation and equivocation.  This is DC's admission-based
current-proposal merge, with the SA seat interface.  The proposer and
the FG action use~$\Sigma.\T$ with DC's filters, as in~\DC{7}.

\paragraph{Frame (FJ on the live caches).}  Let $H=\Sigma.\hmax$ and
$(j,J)=(\Sigma.h_j,\Sigma.J)$.  The store is \emph{contested} when $H=j+1$
and some $W\in\Sigma.\T$ with $\Sigma.\sigma[W].h=H$ does not extend~$J$.
Then~\FJ{2}:
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
 \text{case} & \text{root } Z & \text{band floor } \lo\\
 \hline
 H=j+1,\ \text{not contested} & J & H-1\\
 H=j+1,\ \text{contested} & \Sigma.F & H\\
 H\neq j+1 & \Sigma.F & H-1
\end{array}
\]
The first and the third rows are the root and band of~\DC{5}.  The
contest bit reads the full cumulative tree~$\Sigma.\T$ together with
the live~$\Sigma.\hmax$ and~$\Sigma.J$.  It never reads a view: a contest bit
from a view combined with a cached~$H$ is the mix that~\FJ{2}
forbids~\LDP{1}.  All duties read one frame, from the live caches; the
HEAD applies it to its own processed-block view.  A block $B$ of a
block set~$X$ is \emph{viable} in~$X$ when $\Sigma.F\preceq B$ and some
$W\in X$ with $B\preceq W$ has $\Sigma.\sigma[W].h\geq\lo$.  The
filtered tree of~$X$ is the set of blocks viable in~$X$ that extend~$Z$.
A child of a block with state height below~$\lo$ is eligible without a
majority.  Thus in a contest the band is $\{H\}$ for both viability and
eligibility.  The code must change both: a contest that changes only
one of them can stall the FG for ever~\FJ{7} (N12).  The contest test is
a sufficient local certificate that $J$ cannot finalize; its negation
does not show that $J$ can finalize~\FJ{3}.  Do not replace it by a
test for a visible timeout~\FJ{3} (F4).  Finality acceptance keeps its
own viability test, with the witness height $\Sigma.\hmax-1$ of~\DC{5},
also in a contest~\FJ{2}.

\paragraph{Walk start.}  Every HEAD walk starts at the \emph{live root}: the
deepest live lock target in the \emph{full} filtered tree
$\Call{get\_filtered\_block\_tree}{\Sigma}$, or the FG root~$Z$ when
there is none.  This is $\Call{get\_sg\_root}{}$ of~\DC{7} with the
deepest live lock target in place of the saved grade-1 block: since
the live targets form an ancestor-closed chain, the deepest live target
in the filtered tree is the active prefix of the deepest live
target~\LDP{1},~\cite[\S4]{ALFS}.  The HEAD tests the live root in the full filtered
tree and walks its own filtered view from it.  As in~\cite{DC}, the walk
starts at its start block even when that block is not in the tree that
it walks; with no eligible child in that tree, the head is the start
block~\cite[\S5]{DC}.  A compatible lock outside the full FJ-filtered tree has no
choice authority, but it stays in the gate guard and in the revocation
test.  A lock on an ancestor of~$Z$ is already satisfied.  There is no
unfiltered fallback.  The walk uses the SA score (equivocators in the vote set plus
clean declared supporters above the block) and the eligibility
of~\DC{5} with~$\lo$ in place of $\Sigma.\hmax-1$.

\paragraph{The FG action.}  At~$a_r$, after the gate of slot~$rR$, an
awake validator computes at most one new row~\FAST{3}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $\Cfg$ is the deepest block of the full filtered tree that is in
  $\Sigma.\fastWrite[rR]$ and has a lock deadline after~$a_r$.  The
  latest gate is the scheduled gate at~$d_{rR}$, also when it returned
  no result; no older set replaces an empty one.  $\Cfg$ need not have
  slot~$rR$; its entry $\sigma[\Cfg].T_h$ can be older.
\item If there is no such block, no row is sent.  There is no
  fallback to $S$, to the root, to a slow lock, or to an old fast
  write.  The source rule applies to every height pair: targets
  and timeouts.
\item The finality pair comes from the head of the full admitted tree,
  with the current-slot votes, as in~\DC{7}.  The client recomputes it
  at each action and attaches it only to a new height vote above its height.
\end{itemize}
The node processes its own sent row at once, as in~\DC{7}.

\paragraph{Tick order.}\label{par:tick-order}  The public times are the only tick times.  The
tick at a public time runs once, before the objects that the node
processes at that time.  A tick first advances the clock and removes
the expired locks.  At $q_{s-1}=t_s+\Delta$ it then revokes locks
against the slot-$(s-1)$ votes.  At $d_{s-1}=t_s+2\Delta$ it runs the
gate of slot~$s-1$.  Only then does an awake node run its duties, from
the updated store: the proposal at~$t_s$, the vote at $t_s+\Delta$, and
the FG action at~$a_r$.  Thus the revocation comes before the vote at the
same time, and the gate of slot~$rR$ comes before the FG action at
$a_r=d_{rR}$.  An asleep node runs the same tick and the same handlers,
but no duty.  The node processes its own block and its own vote only on
delivery, as in~\SAS.

\Function{on\_tick}{$\Sigma,\Lambda,t$}
  \Comment{\DC{7}, redefined (b); order of \SAS{} and \ADJ{2}}
  \State $s\gets\lfloor t/(4\Delta)\rfloor$; $(\Sigma.t,\Sigma.s)\gets(t,s)$
  \Comment{no grades}
  \State \Call{expire\_locks}{$\Sigma,t$}

    \Comment{$q_{s-1}$, before the vote}
  \EndIf
  \If{$s>0$ and $t=t_s+2\Delta$}
    \State \Call{update\_confirmation}{$\Sigma,s-1$}
    \Comment{$d_{s-1}$, before the FG action}
  \EndIf

    \State \Call{propose\_block}{$\Sigma$}
  \EndIf

    \Comment{also at slot~$0$ (\SAS)}
  \EndIf

    \State \Call{attest}{$\Sigma,\Lambda$}

  \EndIf

\Function{expire\_locks}{$\Sigma,t$}
  \Comment{\SAS, unchanged}
  \ForAll{$B$ with $\Sigma.\lockDeadline[B]\neq\bot$ and $\Sigma.\lockDeadline[B]\leq t$}
    \State $\Sigma.\lockDeadline[B]\gets\bot$
    \Comment{deadlines are vote times}
  \EndFor
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{revoke\_locks}{$\Sigma,r$}
  \Comment{\SAS; revocation counts use active heads}
  \ForAll{$B$ with $\Sigma.\lockDeadline[B]\neq\bot$}
    \Comment{also locks outside the filtered tree}
    \If{$\Call{revocation\_count}{\Sigma,r,B}\geq\tau$}
      \State $\Sigma.\lockDeadline[B]\gets\bot$
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{update\_confirmation}{$\Sigma,s$}
  \Comment{SA gate; finality rules of \cite[\S2]{FB}}
  \If{$s\notin\dom\Sigma.\savedConfirmation$}
    \State $C\gets\Call{saved\_confirmation\_result}{\Sigma,s}$
    \Comment{at $d_s$; active view}
    \State $\Sigma.\savedConfirmation[s]\gets C$;\quad $\Sigma.\fastWrite[s]\gets\emptyset$
    \Comment{evaluate once, also empty or vetoed}
    \If{$C\neq\bot$ and no $K$ with $\Sigma.\lockDeadline[K]\neq\bot$ conflicts with $C$}
      \Comment{whole-result guard}
      \State $\Sigma.\latestConfirmed\gets
        \Call{advance}{\Sigma.\latestConfirmed,C}$
      \ForAll{$B\in\act(\Sigma)$ with $B\neq\genesis$ and $B\preceq C$}
        \State $\mathit{fast}\gets\Call{fast\_admissible}{\Sigma,s,B}$
        \If{$\mathit{fast}$ or $\Call{slow\_admissible}{\Sigma,s,B}$}
          \State $\Sigma.\lockDeadline[B]\gets\max\{\Sigma.\lockDeadline[B],t_s+6\Delta+L\}$
          \Comment{$\max\{\bot,x\}=x$}
        \EndIf
        \If{$\mathit{fast}$}
          \State add $B$ to $\Sigma.\fastWrite[s]$
          \Comment{a slow renewal alone does not count}
        \EndIf
      \EndFor
    \EndIf
  \EndIf
  \If{$\Sigma.F\not\preceq\Sigma.\latestConfirmed$}
    \State $\Sigma.\latestConfirmed\gets\Sigma.F$
    \Comment{DC output rule; no lock or result write}
  \EndIf
\EndFunction

  \Comment{\DC{7}, unchanged}
  \State \Return $\mathit{old}$ if $C=\bot$ or $C\preceq\mathit{old}$, else $C$
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{get\_finalized}{$\Sigma$}
  \Comment{new, \FAST{4}; user-facing}
  \State \Return $\Sigma.F$
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{get\_confirmed}{$\Sigma$}

  \State \Return $\Sigma.\latestConfirmed$
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{get\_stable}{$\Sigma$}
  \Comment{\DC{7}, redefined (b); no separate stable record}
  \State \Return $\Call{get\_confirmed}{\Sigma}$
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{on\_block}{$\Sigma,B$}
  \Comment{\DC{7}; $B$ passed the input check}
  \If{$B.\slot>\Sigma.s$ or $B\in\Sigma.\T$ or
      $B.\parent\notin\Sigma.\T$} \State \Return \EndIf
  \If{$\Sigma.F\not\preceq B$} \State \Return \EndIf
  \Comment{refuse before unpacking}
  \If{$B.\parent.\slot\geq B.\slot$ or
      the slot-$B.\slot$ proposer key does not sign $B$}
    \State \Return
  \EndIf
  \State $\sigma\gets\Sigma.\sigma[B.\parent]$; $\sigma.s\gets B.\slot$
  \If{some $a\in B.\attsf$ fails $\mathrm{R6W}(a,\sigma)$}
    \State \Return
    \Comment{R6-window; one parent state for all rows}
  \EndIf
  \State $\Sigma.\sigma[B]\gets
    \Call{state\_transition}{\Sigma.\sigma[B.\parent],B}$
  \State add $B$ to $\Sigma.\T$; $\Sigma.\timestamp(B)\gets\Sigma.t$
  \ForAll{$\mathit{vote}\in B.\votesf$}
    \State \Call{on\_goldfish\_vote}{$\Sigma,\mathit{vote}$}
  \EndFor
  \State \Call{update\_finality}{$\Sigma,\Sigma.\sigma[B]$}
  \ForAll{$a\in B.\attsf$}
    \State \Call{on\_attestation}{$\Sigma,a$}
    \Comment{after the finality update, as in DC}
  \EndFor
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{on\_goldfish\_vote}{$\Sigma,\mathit{vote}$}
  \Comment{\SAS, unchanged}
  \State $(i,r,H)\gets\mathit{vote}$
  \If{$r<\Sigma.s-1$ or $r>\Sigma.s$ or
    $\mathit{vote}\in\Sigma.\gfPool[r]$}
    \State \Return \EndIf
  \If{$\Sigma.\gfPool[r]$ holds two distinct votes by seat~$i$}
    \State \Return
  \EndIf
  \State add $\mathit{vote}$ to $\Sigma.\gfPool[r]$;
    $\Sigma.\timestamp(\mathit{vote})\gets\Sigma.t$
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{on\_attestation}{$\Sigma,a$}
  \Comment{INTENTIONAL: checked content only; no DC admission limits}
  \If{$a\in\Sigma.\attestations$} \State \Return \EndIf
  \Comment{one copy per full content}
  \State add $a$ to $\Sigma.\attestations$
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{update\_finality}{$\Sigma,\sigma$}
  \Comment{\DC{7}; SA reconciliation replaces grade clipping}
  \State $\Sigma.\hmax\gets\max(\Sigma.\hmax,\sigma.h)$
  \If{$\Sigma.F\preceq\sigma.J$ \textbf{and}
      $(\sigma.h_j,\sigma.J.\rootf)>(\Sigma.h_j,\Sigma.J.\rootf)$}
    \Comment{lex order}
    \State $(\Sigma.J,\Sigma.h_j)\gets(\sigma.J,\sigma.h_j)$
  \EndIf
  \If{$\Sigma.F\prec\sigma.F$ \textbf{and} $\sigma.F\preceq\Sigma.J$
      \textbf{and} $\sigma.F\in\Call{viable\_tree}{\Sigma}$}
    \State $\Sigma.F\gets\sigma.F$
    \State \Call{reconcile\_with\_finality}{$\Sigma$}
  \EndIf
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{reconcile\_with\_finality}{$\Sigma$}
  \Comment{new (b): \cite[\S2]{FB}; SA analogue of DC clipping and outputs}
  \Comment{$\Sigma.\T$ stays cumulative (\DC{7}); the SA base reads $\act(\Sigma)$}
  \ForAll{$B$ incompatible with $\Sigma.F$ with $\Sigma.\lockDeadline[B]\neq\bot$}
    \State $\Sigma.\lockDeadline[B]\gets\bot$
  \EndFor
  \ForAll{$r\in\dom\Sigma.\fastWrite$}
    \State $\Sigma.\fastWrite[r]\gets
      \{B\in\Sigma.\fastWrite[r]:B\text{ is compatible with }\Sigma.F\}$
  \EndFor
  \ForAll{$r\in\dom\Sigma.\savedConfirmation$ with $\Sigma.\savedConfirmation[r]\neq\bot$}
    \If{$\Sigma.\savedConfirmation[r]$ is incompatible with $\Sigma.F$}
      \State $\Sigma.\savedConfirmation[r]\gets\bot$
      \Comment{keep $r$ evaluated; never rerun this gate}
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \If{$\Sigma.F\not\preceq\Sigma.\latestConfirmed$}
    \State $\Sigma.\latestConfirmed\gets\Sigma.F$
  \EndIf
  \State keep the signed vote and row pools, stamps, $\Lambda$, and $\Sigma.\hmax$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}

The transition folds all rows, finalizes the highest ready window entry,
deletes entries at or below the new $h_F$, then tests target quorum before
mature progress.  A target quorum appends an empty entry and advances once;
mixed progress advances once without an append.  Height advance clears
only target and progress bits.  No row in the block is applied again to
the new entry.  A deleted entry below the new $F$ need not have its own
finality quorum~\cite[\S1]{FW}.

\Function{state\_transition}{$\sigma,B$}
  \Comment{\DC{7}, extended (c): C* proposer key and finality window}
  \State \textbf{assert} the slot-$B.\slot$ proposer key signs $B$
  \Comment{checked in \fname{on\_block}}
  \State \textbf{assert} $B.\parent.\slot<B.\slot$
  \State \textbf{assert} every $a\in B.\attsf$ passes $\mathrm{R6W}(a,\sigma)$
  \Comment{same parent post-state, before any row effect}
  \State $\sigma.s\gets B.\slot$
  \ForAll{$a\in B.\attsf$}
    \State $\sigma\gets\Call{process\_attestation}{\sigma,a}$
  \EndFor
  \State $\sigma.L\gets B$
  \State \Return $\Call{process\_height\_events}{\sigma}$
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{process\_attestation}{$\sigma,a$}
  \Comment{\DC{7}, extended (c): independent height and finality effects}
  \State $\valindex\gets a.\valindex$
  \ForAll{$(j,X,Q_i)\in\sigma.\mathcal{W}$ with $a$'s finality pair $=(j,X)$}
    \State $Q_i\gets Q_i\cup\{\valindex\}$ in $\sigma.\mathcal{W}$
  \EndFor
  \If{$a$'s height pair is $(h,T,\chi)$ with
      $h=\sigma.h$ and $T=\sigma.T_h$}
    \Comment{names the chain's own entry}
    \State $\sigma.\progressBit[\valindex]\gets\mathsf{true}$
    \If{not $\chi$}
      \State $\sigma.\targetParticipation[\valindex]\gets\mathsf{true}$
    \EndIf
  \EndIf
  \State \Return $\sigma$
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{process\_height\_events}{$\sigma$}
  \Comment{\DC{7}, extended (c): unit quorums and finality window}
  \If{some $(j,X,Q_i)\in\sigma.\mathcal{W}$ has $j>\sigma.h_F$ and $|Q_i|\geq q_F$}
    \Comment{finality first}
    \State select such an entry with greatest $j$
    \State $(\sigma.F,\sigma.h_F)\gets(X,j)$
  \EndIf
  \State delete from $\sigma.\mathcal{W}$ all entries with $j\leq\sigma.h_F$
  \If{$|\Qtarget(\sigma)|\geq q_F$}
    \If{$\neg\sigma.\nj$}
      \Comment{always true: $\nj=\mathsf{false}$ (a)}
      \State $(\sigma.J,\sigma.h_j)\gets(\sigma.T_h,\sigma.h)$
      \State append $(\sigma.h,\sigma.T_h,\emptyset)$ to $\sigma.\mathcal{W}$
      \State retain only the newest $K_W$ entries of $\sigma.\mathcal{W}$
    \EndIf
    \State \Return $\Call{advance\_height}{\sigma}$
  \EndIf
  \If{$|\Qprog(\sigma)|\geq q_F$ \textbf{and}
      $\sigma.s\geq\sigma.T_h.\slot+\delta_{\mathrm{t}}$}
    \Comment{timeouts count only after the delay; no window append or clear}
    \State \Return $\Call{advance\_height}{\sigma}$
  \EndIf
  \State \Return $\sigma$
  \Comment{any child can consume a quorum}
\EndFunction
\vspace{1pt}
\Function{advance\_height}{$\sigma$}
  \Comment{\DC{7}, redefined (a) by \FJ{2}: no NJ latch}
  \State $\sigma.h\gets\sigma.h+1$; \quad $\sigma.T_h\gets\sigma.L$
  \State $\sigma.\nj\gets\mathsf{false}$
  \Comment{\cite{DC}: $(K\mid\sigma.h)\wedge(\sigma.h-\sigma.h_F>D)$}
  \State $\sigma.\targetParticipation,
    \sigma.\progressBit\gets\mathsf{false}^{V}$
  \State \Return $\sigma$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}

  \Comment{the full filtered tree, not a view}

  \Comment{they lie on one chain}

The SA gate helpers use the active view~$\act(\Sigma)$ and the

the saved confirmation is $\bot$.  Revocation and early-support
counts require heads in the active view; late participation and
equivocation do not require resolution.  Slow tests use the saved

    \Comment{locked elsewhere: wait out the height}
  \EndIf

      \State \Return $(h_c,T_c,\mathsf{false})$

    \EndIf

  \If{$T_f\neq\bot$} \State $\Lambda.\mathsf{lock}[h_f]\gets T_f$ \EndIf
  \If{$h\neq\bot$}
    \If{not $\chi$ and $\Lambda.\mathsf{target}[h]=\bot$}
      \State $\Lambda.\mathsf{target}[h]\gets T$
    \EndIf
    \If{$\chi$}
      \State $\Lambda.\mathsf{timeout}[h]\gets\mathsf{true}$;
        add $(h,T)$ to $\Lambda.\mathsf{timeouts}$
    \EndIf
  \EndIf
\EndFunction

\section{Leak identification}\label{sec:leak-identification}

The leak ledger is derived from one chain.  Height events still occur
once per block, in Figure~\ref{alg:chain-full}.  The ledger settles once
per round, separately in each of three layers.  It changes no validator
weight and enters no quorum, fork-choice, or state-transition rule.
The penalty schedule is outside this document.  The analysis and
marker repair are given in~\cite{RL179,RLR,LS183,LSR}.
The cut and closing-marker rule in~\cite{LS183} supersede those
in~\cite{RL179}.

\paragraph{Changes from DC.}
The form follows~\DC{8}, with five accounting changes.
First, DC charges at each block; Goldilocks settles once per round, as the
user requires.  For $\kappa=2$, the cut includes the receipt-ready +$\kappa$
carrier at every $R\geq2$
and later same-round inclusions at $R>3$~\cite[\S2]{LS183}.
Second, DC multiplies the charge by the slot gap
$d=B.\slot-B.\parent.\slot$; Goldilocks adds one unit per crossed
settlement boundary in each active layer.  Each boundary has its own
reads, so the last charge cannot represent all skipped intervals.
Third, DC reads the markers before each block's height events.
Goldilocks compares boundary post-states and normally reads the closing
markers.  This counts progress over the full interval, instead of
charging a reset context at each block.  The old-pair read below
protects finality rows included before the first pair replacement,
including rows in that replacement block~\cite[\S\S2--3]{LS183}.
Fourth, DC excludes waiting from its stalled-block bound.  The requested
per-round state bound has no waiting exception: even when $L_1$ is
empty, $L_{2t}$ or $L_{2f}$ supplies the bound~\cite[\S3.2]{RL179}.
Fifth, DC's $L_{2f}$ requires a pair pending before the block and thus
excuses a block that opens a new pair.  Goldilocks tests the closing gap.
A transition exemption would allow a round with no finality and no
charge; the alternative in~\cite[\S\S1,3.3,6.1]{RL179} gives only
$(m-1)(n-q_F)$ in unit weights over $m$ rounds without finality.  The user instead chose
``finalize or at least one third leaked'' at each settlement as a pure
state property, and permits charges caused by proposer omissions
\cite[\S1]{LS183}.  The exact unit-weight bound below is greater than
$n/3$.  These choices change DC's accounting policy; a round multiplier
alone with DC's other tests does not suffice~\cite[\S6.1]{RL179}.
Goldilocks also has no NJ exemption because its NJ field is always false.
Its fairness theorem covers all three layers only in the stated strong
window; DC's fairness statement covers $L_1$ under its source-distance
premise~\DC{8}.

\paragraph{Settlement cut.}
For integer $R\geq2$, put
\[
 \kappa_{\rm leak}=\max\{2,R-1\},\qquad
 s^{\rm leak}_r=rR+\kappa_{\rm leak}=\max\{rR+2,(r+1)R-1\}.
\]
Settle round~$r$ after slot~$s^{\rm leak}_r$, including that slot's block and
its height events, if that block is on the chain.  Let $\Xi_r$ be the
post-state of the last block at slot at most~$s^{\rm leak}_r$.
Use $\Xi_0$ as an uncharged anchor; charge only $r\geq1$.
The accounting interval is $(s^{\rm leak}_{r-1},s^{\rm leak}_r]$, of length~$R$.
For $\kappa=2$, at $R=2$ the cut is the next opening; that block also serves as
the carrier for round~$r$.  At $R=3$, it is both the round end and the carrier.
At $R>3$, the cut is the round end and permits further inclusion
after the carrier when $\kappa=2$.  For $\kappa>2$, the receipt-ready
carrier is $rR+\kappa$ and can lie after this cut when $\kappa=R$.
Keep $\kappa_{\rm leak}$ unchanged; fairness and HP zero-charge bounds
for $\kappa>2$ are \OPEN~\cite[F5]{FPR}.
A missing block copies the last post-state;
it causes no height event.  No row from a later slot enters $\Xi_r$.

\paragraph{Marker reads.}
Write $\Xi_-=\Xi_{r-1}$ and $\Xi_+=\Xi_r$.  For a block~$D$, let
$\sigma_{\rm pre}(D)$ be its parent's state with its slot set to
$D.\slot$, its rows folded in, and $L$ set to~$D$, just before
the height events.  Thus this read includes rows in~$D$; it is not
the parent's post-state.
If $\Xi_-.h_F<\Xi_-.h_j$, $\Xi_+.h_F=\Xi_-.h_F$, and $\Xi_+.h_j>\Xi_-.h_j$, let $D_r$
be the first block on the chain in $(s^{\rm leak}_{r-1},s^{\rm leak}_r]$ that replaces $\Xi_-$'s justified
pair.  Define
\[
 Q_{\rm leak}(r)=\begin{cases}
 \Qfin(\sigma_{\rm pre}(D_r)),
   & \Xi_-.h_F<\Xi_-.h_j,\ \Xi_+.h_F=\Xi_-.h_F,\ \Xi_+.h_j>\Xi_-.h_j,\\
 \Qfin(\Xi_+),&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The first case reads the old latest pair's markers before the replacement
changes the latest-pair read.  The window can retain those markers.  It uses the first such block even if later blocks replace
the pair again.  If no pair is pending at~$\Xi_-$, use the closing markers.
All these reads are derived from ancestors, not from live FG caches.

The three identified sets are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 L_1(r)&=V\setminus\Qprog(\Xi_+)
   &&\text{if $\Xi_+.h=\Xi_-.h$, else $\emptyset$;}\\
 L_{2t}(r)&=V\setminus\Qtarget(\Xi_+)
   &&\text{if $\Xi_+.h_j=\Xi_-.h_j$, else $\emptyset$;}\\
 L_{2f}(r)&=V\setminus Q_{\rm leak}(r)
   &&\text{if $\Xi_+.h_F=\Xi_-.h_F<\Xi_+.h_j$, else $\emptyset$.}
\end{aligned}
\]
A member receives one unit in each active layer.  Membership in two
layers gives two units.  For tightness, count each identity once in
$\mathcal U_r=L_1(r)\cup L_{2t}(r)\cup L_{2f}(r)$.
The closing-gap test in $L_{2f}$ is strict: a round that creates a
pending pair without a finality advance is charged, even if no action
could yet read that pair.  There is no transition-round exemption.

  \Comment{floor toward $-\infty$}
\EndFunction

  \State \Return $\Sigma.\sigma[C]$

  \State $\iota_-\gets\Call{settlement\_index}{X.\parent.\slot}$
  \State $\iota_+\gets\Call{settlement\_index}{X.\slot}$

    \State $(L_1,L_{2t},L_{2f})\gets\Call{round\_leak\_sets}{\Sigma,X,r}$
    \State $\ell\gets\ell+(\mathbf1_{v\in L_1},\mathbf1_{v\in L_{2t}},

The loop counts settlement boundaries, not slots.  Evaluate each crossed
boundary with its own snapshots and replacement read; do not multiply
the last charge by the number of skipped rounds.  Equal terms can be
batched only after these reads agree.  Ancestor states and one ancestor
pre-state suffice for the marker repair.  A client that prunes ancestors
must retain enough authenticated data to reproduce these reads and the
ledger, including the three counters at the preceding boundary.
This is a storage obligation, not a new height-event rule.
For an empty tail at a specified slot horizon, use the last post-state
at every further boundary.  This extension is relative to that chain;
receipt of an old ancestor does not create a new duty.

\paragraph{Tightness.}
On every valid chain from genesis, for each charged round~$r$,
\[
 \Xi_r.h_F>\Xi_{r-1}.h_F\quad\text{or}\quad
 |\mathcal U_r|>n-q_F,\qquad q_F=\lceil2n/3\rceil.
\]
This is a state-transition property.  It requires no synchrony, honesty,
or bound on faulty identities.  It includes empty intervals, waiting,
and progress without finality.  With integer counts the lower bound is
$n-q_F+1>n/3$.  Over $m$ consecutive charged rounds with no finality
advance, $\sum_r|\mathcal U_r|\geq m(n-q_F+1)$; identities can be counted again
in different rounds.  Here finality means the chain counter $h_F$,
not a live node's receipt or acceptance time.

To see the bound, every post-state has
$h_F\leq h_j<h$, $|\Qtarget|<q_F$, and, when $h_F<h_j$,
$|\Qfin|<q_F$.  A quorum is consumed before that post-state is returned.
If finality does not advance and the closing pair is pending, $L_{2f}$
has the required size.  For its repaired read, the old pair was pending
and its finality test failed before the first replacement, so
$|Q_{\rm leak}(r)|<q_F$ there too.  If the closing pair is not pending and finality does not advance,
monotonicity gives $\Xi_-.h_j=\Xi_+.h_j=\Xi_+.h_F=\Xi_-.h_F$; $L_{2t}$ gives the bound.

\paragraph{Fairness and its scope.}
Assume $\Delta_F\leq2\Delta$, Recovery, the full $\mathrm{HEALING\_INPUT}(t^*)$ of
Section~\ref{sec:healing-interface} (including H2$'$), and a strong
round~$r$ with $T_{r-2}\geq t^*$.
By definition, the proposers at $(r-2)R$, $(r-1)R$, $rR$, $rR+1$, and $rR+2$
are honest and perform their required duties.  Here the receipt-ready
carrier is $rR+\kappa=rR+2$.  Define the canonical
chain $\mathcal K$ as the ancestor closure of healed honest outputs; H4 orders
those outputs~\cite[\S5]{LS183}.  On $\mathcal K$ and its continuations through
the honest carrier, every honest validator receives $(0,0,0)$
at settlement~$r$.  In fact all three sets are empty.
The high-record result below ensures that no honest target, timeout,
or lock record blocks the useful target or finality vote
\cite[FF2]{BPF},~\cite[\S4]{SL175}.
The strong-window proof~\cite[FF3--FF4]{BPF} makes all three counters
advance by the +$\kappa$ carrier ($\kappa=2$).  The preceding cut is at or before the
central opening, and the closing cut is at or after the carrier.
Monotonicity proves the claim also for $R=2$ and $R>3$.
Equivalently, under Recovery and the full interface, a charge on $\mathcal K$
at settlement $r$ with $T_{r-2}\geq t^*$ requires a faulty proposer
in at least one of $(r-2)R$, $(r-1)R$, $rR$, $rR+1$, and $rR+2$.
This is the user's permitted proposer-omission scope; it does not
identify the charged validator as faulty.

\paragraph{HP premises and zero charges.}
For $\Delta_F\leq2\Delta$, HP assumes $a=0$, all nodes awake, strict receipt and delivery, an
honest proposal in every slot from $r_0R$, and the full healing
interface for the chosen $\delta_{\rm t}\geq0$, with $R\geq2$~\cite[HP]{BPF}.  Here $r_0$ is the start round with
$T_{r_0}\geq t^*$, whose opening $Q_0$ has height $d\geq M+1$.
HP's start and history premises 1--2 require that every block emitted
before $T_{r_0}$ is an ancestor of $Q_0$, no height row at height at
least $d$ is emitted before $a_{r_0}$, and every honest record at
height $y\geq d$ is $(\bot,\mathsf{false},\bot)$ just before
$T_{r_0}$; $\Lambda.\mathsf{timeouts}$ and $\Lambda.\mathsf{sent}$
contain no entry at height $y\geq d$.  Under these premises, all settlements $r\geq r_0+2$
have zero charges~\cite[\S5]{LS183}.  These are stronger premises
than Recovery.  For other timeout delays, the interface is supplied
separately; this is not a GST recovery result.

This fairness statement does not cover every round after stabilization.
A faulty proposer can omit a row.  The ledger can charge the omitted row's
honest signer when the relevant counter has not advanced and no other
inclusion sets its marker by the cut.  At $R=2$, one faulty slot can also
leave an honest row with a stale context in the old transition-only
example (the stale-context trace of~\cite[\S6, M2]{LS183},
from the round-leak review~\cite{RLR}).  Its stale-height inclusion
needs a new R6-window check; a stale row is valid only with a matching
retained finality pair.
A later transition can charge honest identities after an earlier faulty
proposer, even when the later proposers include all their rows.
A timeout-only crossing advances $h$ without justification and clears
$\Qtarget$.  If that set is still empty at settlement and $h_j$ has
not advanced in the interval, $L_{2t}=V$: the target layer charges every
identity, including honest validators whose rows were included before
the crossing~\cite[\S3.2]{RL179}.  Later matching target rows can
reduce this set.  The marker repair protects included finality rows
for the old pending pair at the first replacement; it does not remove
these target-layer charges.  Reading the target set before the first
height-advancing block is an \OPEN{} design option, not the adopted
rule (Section~\ref{sec:open}).

\begin{remark}[Necessary honest charges, M4 of~\cite{RLR,LS183}]
Let $V_{\rm bad}$ be the faulty set and suppose
$|V_{\rm bad}|\leq n-q_F$.  Any ledger with the stated strict per-round
bound must identify an honest validator in every charged round with
no finality advance.  Indeed,
\[
 |\mathcal U_r\setminus V_{\rm bad}|
 \ \geq\ |\mathcal U_r|-|V_{\rm bad}|
 \ \geq\ n-q_F+1-|V_{\rm bad}|\ \geq\ 1.
\]
The Recovery fault bound implies this premise.  The conclusion also
holds when all rows are included.  Thus strict tightness is not a
proof that each charge identifies a faulty validator or a missed duty.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[DC only]
Goldilocks has no NJ heights and has no NJ leak guard.  For a corresponding
round ledger in DC, the NJ refinement reads the closing state
$\Xi_+=\Xi_r$: when $\Xi_+.\nj=\mathsf{true}$, it charges $L_{2t}$ only if
$\Xi_+.h_F=\Xi_-.h_F$.  This retains the strict tightness
argument, which uses $L_{2t}$ only in that case.  It is a remark about
DC, not a Goldilocks rule.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{High records and useful votes.}
Under the full healing interface and its execution premises, let
$y\geq M+1$ have an honest source, and let $E_y$ be that source's
entry.  At every reachable honest record read,
\[
 \Lambda.\mathsf{target}[y]\in\{\bot,E_y\},\qquad
 \Lambda.\mathsf{timeout}[y]=\mathsf{false},\qquad
 \Lambda.\mathsf{lock}[y]\in\{\bot,E_y\}.
\]
If no honest source exists at that height, its honest records are
empty.  Thus, after $t^*$, no honest target, timeout, or lock record
at a high height blocks a useful vote for the source entry or its
head-derived finality pair~\cite[FF2]{BPF},~\cite[\S4, E-ST]{SL175}.
This claim concerns only the three displayed record fields.
The sent set and the $h>h_f$ rule can suppress a row or its finality
pair; see the FGoodE deadline in Section~\ref{sec:recurrence}.
The client comment ``locked elsewhere: wait out the height'' in
Figure~\ref{alg:client} cannot suppress these high votes.  It still
applies to an unprotected old context; the result does not erase old
records or create a voting duty before its scheduled action.
For SA locks, H3 of Section~\ref{sec:healing-interface} states that
the whole-result guard passes at each covered honest proposal and
that the gate writes its fast lock.  Thus those locks also do not
block the covered useful source.  The two results have separate
premises: FG record feasibility does not itself prove SA service.

\subsection{Healing interface}\label{sec:healing-interface}

The finality proofs use $\mathrm{HEALING\_INPUT}(t^*)$ of
\cite[H.22]{BPH}, also stated in~\cite[\S1]{BPF}.
It consists of healing from~$t^*$ and the following clauses with one
witness tuple $(r_G,u_0,M,r^*,s^*,b,C^*,u,Q^*)$.
Write $\operatorname{ht}(B)=\sigma[B].h$.  A high source, row, entry,
or certificate has height at least $M+1$.
Here $\mu=\mu(\mathrm{run},t_{\rm GST})$ is a function of the run
and GST, not an existential witness field.  It has the value defined
in H0 below, as in~\cite[H.11]{BPH}.
The blueprint names are used.  H2 and H2.A together give source
transfer.  T-ROWS, H5, and H6 are proved services of the same package~\cite[H.22]{BPH}.  They use the same
witness tuple and event reads.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item[H0: cut and reset.]  Use the inclusive reset cut defined above.
  All honest height rows through $u_0$ have height at most $M$.
  Also $\mu\leq M$, where $\mu$ is the maximum of 1 and the heights
  of honest rows emitted at actions $a_r$ whose source vote time
  $v_{rR}$ is before GST.
  $t_{\rm GST}+2P\leq u_0<t_{\rm GST}+3P$.
  Set $b=s^*$.  The block $C^*$ is an actual fresh source of an honest
  row at height $M+1$ emitted at action $a_{r^*}$; $\operatorname{ht}(C^*)=M+1$
  and $v_{s^*}\geq t_{\rm GST}$.  The first honest proposal slot
  $u\geq s^*+2$ has block $Q^*$; $t^*=d_u\geq t_{\rm GST}$ and
  $C^*\preceq Q^*$.
\item[H1: actual sources.]  A source is the block selected by
  \fname{get\_fg\_vote} from the opening-label $\fastWrite$ set with
  a live lock in the full filtered tree.  Include selections whose

\item[H3: exact service.]  For every honest proposal $Q$ at slot
  $v\geq s^*+2$, all honest slot-$v$ HEADs equal $Q$.  At $d_v$, each
  honest raw saved confirmation is $Q$, its whole-result guard passes,

\item[H5: raw-result transfer.]  Under Recovery, every nonempty honest raw saved confirmation $C$

\begin{theorem}[Outage continuation under (V)]\label{thm:outage-continuation}
Fix an honest reader~$x$ and $B\preceq S_x(T)$.  Assume the Outage
continuation premise set, with its fast or slow lag and
\begin{equation}
 b_1-b_0+\bigl((b_0-6\Delta)\bmod 4\Delta\bigr)\leq L. \tag{V}
\end{equation}
For every honest~$y$ and $b_0\leq t\leq\mathrm{horizon}$,
\[
 B\preceq S_y(t),\qquad B\preceq\mathrm{stable}_y(t),\qquad
 B\preceq\mathrm{confirmed}_y(t),\qquad B\parallel F_y(t).
\]
Here $\parallel$ is prefix compatibility and reads include all events
at~$t$.  Honest HEADs can regress below~$B$; the conclusion concerns
the available outputs and compatibility of finality.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Genesis is immediate.  Otherwise the healthy prefix and lag give
$6\Delta\leq b_0$.  Put
\[
 j=\left\lfloor\frac{b_0-6\Delta}{4\Delta}\right\rfloor+1,
 \quad e_0=d_j+L,\quad r=\min\{s:b_1\leq v_s\}.
\]
Condition~(V) gives $r\leq j+k$ and $v_r<e_0$.  Arming and the
pre-expiry induction protect~$B$ through the old gates.  The first
restored HEAD batch therefore extends~$B$, before lock expiry.
At the boundary $r=j+k$, $v_{r+1}=e_0$ but $d_r=e_0+\Delta$;
the proof does not assume a restored gate before the next HEAD.

Induct over actual events and scheduled HEAD labels.  A first HEAD
regression supplies a justified entry above~$B$.  Acceptance relay
makes its carrier available to all honest readers before the next
HEAD.  Window retention then preserves HEAD compatibility, including
after the contest ends.  The full active-store gate count excludes
a conflicting result.  Each new lock is a prefix of the current
passed result; an old saved confirmation cannot create a conflicting
lock.  Certificate origins preserve $F\parallel B$.  The output
invariant gives $B\preceq S$, also at sleeping receivers, and both
public outputs equal~$S$.
The full proof is in \nolinkurl{drafts/outage-output-direct.md},
\S\S1--10; timing and the boundary run are in
\nolinkurl{drafts/outage-persist-reserve.md}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Start of the confirmation walk.}

At every completed event the paired executions have equal external
traces: outputs, honest messages, and honest duty choices agree.
Their evaluated labels agree, as do their locks, exact deadlines,
and fast-write sets on descendants of the current~$F$.
Local differences can include an omitted saved ancestor result,
omitted ancestor lock writes and fast-write entries at a dropped gate,
and lost slow renewals on ancestors of~$F$ at a retained gate.
Such ancestors cannot affect a later choice, since $F$ moves only
forward.  The prefix-write loop and finality reconciliation do not change.
Every output-level theorem and bound therefore transfers under the same
premises.  For the conditional general-delay results, the new H3 local
clauses imply the old H3 clauses in the paired execution: a nonempty
new result is the same old result, and its guard, fast write, and FG
source agree.  As a Recovery conclusion, H3 transfers through the
blueprint lemma that an active honest slot-$s$ proposal $P$ has
$F\prec P$ at its gate (foundations, Section~6.6): the new raw result,
its fast write, and $C_{\mathrm{fg}}=Q$ then equal the old ones.

\paragraph{First crossing without a row round.}\label{par:first-crossing}

  $\tau$.  No honest earlier HEAD can cover $C$ or a descendant,
  for any declared slot of $C$.

  frames, so the below-floor arm fails at $C$.
  Acceptance (\nameref{par:acceptance})
  requires slot at most $z+2$ and the slot proposer's

  $h=n-a>a$.  Strict majority would require $p_C>h$, which is impossible.
\item The walk cannot start beyond $C$.  Lower cached roots precede

  bounds for this window revision remain \OPEN{} until the window-fold check.
  QF1--QF5 settle only the old-fold qualified-recurrence and opening-deletion
  cases stated in Section~\ref{sec:recurrence}, at $R=2,3$.  For
  $R\geq4$, the negatives QF1, QF3 and QF5 are not proved; QF2 and QF4
  are dropped open extensions, outside the claim set; their retained arguments hold for the old fold with $\Delta_F\leq2\Delta$ and the Tail premises
  above for every $R\geq2$~\cite[\S9]{BPF},~\cite{NRB,NRBC}.
\item \OPEN: \emph{tight local healing bounds.}
  The local deadline $q_{s^*}+\Gamma+5\Delta$ is attained at $k=13$
  in N.1.  Tightness for small~$L$ and other timeout delays remains
  open~\cite[N.1, \S11]{BPH}.  The uniform limitations in
  Section~\ref{sec:status} include both $R=2$ and $R=3$.
\item \OPEN: \emph{leak implementation and scope.}
  Section~\ref{sec:leak-identification} defines the strict ledger.
  The penalty schedule and a pruning method that preserves its reads
  remain outside this specification.  The stale-context trace
  (\cite[\S6, M2]{LS183}) is an old transition-only example; its
  stale-height inclusion needs a new R6-window check.
  One \OPEN{} design option reads $\Qtarget$ after the row fold and
  before the height events of the first height-advancing block in the
  settlement interval, instead of using the closing target set.  When
  $h_j$ does not advance, that block's target test failed, so this read
  is below quorum and retains the strict size bound.  If there is no
  such block, it would keep the closing read.  This option could protect
  included target rows from a timeout reset.  Its full fairness and
  implementation effects are not analyzed in the sources.  It is not
  adopted; Figure~\ref{alg:leak} retains the closing target read.
\item \OPEN: \emph{row-pool pruning.}
  One candidate rule drops a row only when it can set bits of entries
  whose checkpoint is an ancestor-or-equal of $\Sigma.F$, and no height bit.
  This rule is not adopted.  Let an admitted block $X$ have state height 4
  and retain $(2,U,Q_2)$.  An honest row has height 3 and finality pair
  $(2,U)$, with its bit absent from $Q_2$.  A later descendant can finalize
  $X$, so the local cache becomes $\Sigma.F=X$.  A new child of $X$ still
  passes admission and uses immutable $\sigma[X]$.  The row passes the
  stale arm ($2<3<4$) and can change its bits and leak markers, although
  $U\preceq\Sigma.F$.  Finality coverage does not prove that these state
  effects are equivalent.  Pruning needs the row-service and marker-read
  proof stated in Section~\ref{sec:complete-store}.
\item \OPEN: \emph{old-pair leak markers after replacement.}
  One design option takes the union of the old pair's markers at $D_r$
  with the same pair's retained window bits at $\Xi_+$; an absent entry
  supplies the empty set.  This could include stale rows carried after
  $D_r$.  Without an $h_F$ advance, a retained pending entry has fewer
  than $q_F$ bits.  Its bits contain the earlier read; if the entry was
  removed by capacity, the union keeps only that earlier read.  Thus the
  union stays below quorum and keeps the strict size bound.  Its fairness
  benefit for $\kappa>2$ needs a proof.  This option is not adopted;
  Figure~\ref{alg:leak} keeps the first-replacement read.
\item \OPEN: \emph{window retention and capacity.}
  A selected checkpoint $U$ survives until carrier $c$ if
  $K_W\geq1+N(U,c)$, where $N$ counts newer justifications before $c$'s
  finality test.  In the healed setting, start from a fresh unanimous
  action with $U$ latest.  Under its causal cap and an opening recurrence
  of gap $g$, $K_{\rm rec}=g+2$ keeps $U$ until carriage or finality
  coverage when $R\geq\kappa$.  The prescribed receipt-ready carriers need only $K_W=2$.
  Do not infer retention across arbitrary proposer gaps from $K_W=4$.
  The slow-receipt negative with $P=24$, $\Delta_F=101$, and $K_W=4$
  keeps finality at genesis but violates $R\geq\kappa$.
  The faulty-withholding segment can evict a selected checkpoint
  before an honest carrier; its later descendant finalizes.  This is
  not a proof that every fixed $K_W$ loses eventual ledger finality.
  A separate mixed crossing can lose a target before its complete
  batch arrives; no window size repairs that target loss~\cite[\S3]{FW}.
  Block capacity, aggregation, row-pool pruning, and persistent window
  storage need separate work.  Complete row-carrying blocks still use $\Delta$.
\item \OPEN: \emph{large-delay proof ports.}
  The action-$r$ batch, qualified healing, and sharper healing bounds
  are now PROVED-paper in blueprint 21.  The dropped QF2/QF4 extensions at $\kappa>2$ need the stronger gap condition
  above or a new proof at $\delta_{\rm t}=2R$.
  Leak fairness, HP zero charges and indexed latency, and the old strong
  alternatives remain open for $\kappa>2$; $\kappa_{\rm leak}$ is unchanged.
  Recheck old negative executions under R6-window and window retention.
\item \OPEN: \emph{Lean.}  There is no checked mixed Goldilocks Lean
  instance~\LDP{7} (X3).  The SA and DC Lean
  theorems verify their own protocols only.  The DC form lets the port
  reuse DC's live-cache lemmas; the new parts are the FJ contest case and
  the lock walk start~\LDP{7} (O7), the $F$ start of the confirmation
  walk and its correspondence proof, plus reconciliation and the
  admission-or-coverage proof of~\cite[\S\S2--7]{FB}.  The combined
  filtered Goldilocks proof remains open.
\item \emph{SA Lean under the receipt premise} (closed).  The
  available-chain merge \texttt{1a3f73a} puts the SA, Grid~CO, and SA-PC
  theorems on the receipt premise; the public theorems keep their
  statements~\cite[\S9]{RR}.  The plain SA base has no FG, so the
  filtered base does not change it~\cite[\S1]{FB}.
\item \OPEN: \emph{retention and missed ticks.}  As in~\SAS: no pruning
  rule for the vote pool, for the row pool (retained without clock expiry), or for blocks of~$\Sigma.\T$ (a pruning rule must keep every later read~\cite[M1]{FR}), and
  no rule for a node that misses a public time.
\item \OPEN: \emph{smaller committees.}  All claims use the equal-size
  profile ($n$ seats per slot and $n$ FG identities).  A sampled smaller
  committee needs separate counts~\FAST{1}.
\end{enumerate}
